**The Role of Crowdsourcing in Public Health**
[Iwaspoisoned.com](https://iwaspoisoned.com/page/about) partners with over 400 public health organizations. Our site utilizes crowdsourced reports of individuals' foodborne illness experiences to aid public health organizations and enhance food safety in their respective areas. By allowing consumers to report instances of foodborne illness, the platform serves as a crucial link between the public and health authorities. This method of crowdsourcing is instrumental in public health as it provides real-time, geo-fenced data that might otherwise go unreported, offering a comprehensive view of food safety issues as they arise.


**Visualizing Our U.S. Public Health Subscribers**
Our map shows the 400+ public health organizations subscribed to us across the United States. These organizations range from county to state health departments, all utilizing our platform to stay informed about local food safety concerns.
**Network Impact**
The partnership between Iwaspoisoned and public health entities has proven beneficial in contributing to the overall improvement in food safety management. Our role in these efforts is primarily as an information conduit, connecting reported incidents with those who can investigate and respond.

**Education, Outreach, and Our Future Focus in Food Safety**
We strengthen our relationships with public health agencies and their communities by presenting insights about food safety and the effectiveness of crowdsourcing at various conferences, educational events, and symposiums. We have participated in more than 50 global events, interacting with and educating public health departments, county health officials, food safety professionals, environmental health specialists, and more. Read about our past and upcoming events[here](https://iwaspoisoned.com/events) .


Our ongoing goal is to continue growing the reach of our public health partnerships. Our efforts reflect our continuous commitment to improving support for public health through our platform.
