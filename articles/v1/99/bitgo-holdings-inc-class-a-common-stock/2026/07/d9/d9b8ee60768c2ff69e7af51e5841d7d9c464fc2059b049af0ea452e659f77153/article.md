# Institutional Crypto Custody: A Guide for Asset Managers

[Institutional crypto custody](https://www.bitgo.com/solutions/investors/) is the third-party safekeeping of digital assets on behalf of[asset managers](https://www.bitgo.com/solutions/asset-managers/) ,[banks](https://www.bitgo.com/solutions/banks/) ,[RIAs](https://www.bitgo.com/products/wealth-management/) , and other[regulated entities](https://www.bitgo.com/entities/) , using infrastructure designed to meet fiduciary, regulatory, and operational standards.


But how do[asset managers](https://www.bitgo.com/solutions/asset-managers/) choose the right custody partner?


The scale of institutional holdings makes them a target for hackers and cybercriminals; in 2024 alone,[cryptocurrency losses reported to the FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/news/press-releases/fbi-releases-annual-internet-crime-report) totaled over $6.5 billion. Pair cybersecurity risk with fiduciary obligations and the irreversibility of blockchain transactions, and one could forgive[asset managers](https://www.bitgo.com/solutions/asset-managers/) for hesitating to enter the digital asset market.


The right[institutional crypto custody](https://www.bitgo.com/solutions/investors/) provider should put you at ease, handling the heavy lifting of securing digital assets and critical functions, including compliance, liquidity, key management, audits, and reporting.


Here's a closer look at how crypto custody works at a larger scale and what to look for in best-in-class custodians.

Choosing a discount provider or trying to do it yourself can lead to costly mistakes, government action, or falling victim to cybercrime.

## How Institutional Crypto Custody Works


Imagine a hedge fund with $100 million in digital assets. They need liquidity for day-to-day trading operations, as well as a secure solution for storing long-term assets.


How does institutional digital asset custody work from that perspective?


Day-to-day trading continues to function smoothly. Custom APIs integrate custodial infrastructure with existing systems so that[asset managers](https://www.bitgo.com/solutions/asset-managers/) retain a unified view of all holdings without the need for parallel digital asset trading or record-keeping systems.


Behind the scenes, however, quite a few systems are working to maintain the balance between secure asset storage and convenient operations.


The first layer is private key management, where custodians generate cryptographic keys within the hardware security modules (HSMs) already familiar to traditional finance. Keys never exist outside these tamper-resistant devices, access requires multiple layers of authentication, and key generation follows documented, auditable protocols.


Wallet infrastructure is the next layer.


The bulk of institutional holdings sit in[cold storage](https://www.bitgo.com/products/cold-wallets/) , kept offline and safely disconnected from the internet, while[hot wallets](https://www.bitgo.com/products/hot-wallets/) hold smaller balances for day-to-day liquidity needs. The split between online and offline storage reduces exposure:[cold wallets](https://www.bitgo.com/resources/blog/protecting-your-digital-assets-cold-wallets-and-their-benefits/) protect against cyber threats,[hot wallets](https://www.bitgo.com/products/hot-wallets/) enable timely and convenient trading.


The next step is to ensure no single point of failure can put assets at risk.


[Multi-sig wallets](https://www.bitgo.com/products/custody-wallets/) require multiple parties to sign off before a trade executes. Private key management is also distributed across multiple parties: TSS-MPC (threshold signature scheme multi-party computation) replaces single-key storage by fragmenting private keys into encrypted “shares” distributed across multiple devices.


From here, transaction approval workflows reinforce technological safeguards. With role-based permissions,[asset managers](https://www.bitgo.com/solutions/asset-managers/) can decide who initiates, approves, or releases transactions.


Reporting, reconciliation, and audit infrastructure round out[qualified custody](https://www.bitgo.com/products/qualified-custody/) infrastructure. Institutions should expect real-time balance visibility, transaction history, and auditable statements suitable for accounting and tax purposes. Furthermore, it is often wise of institutions to narrow their search to SOC 2 Type II compliant organizations; doing so ensures their institutional digital asset custody partner adheres to one of the highest standards of security practices.


These mechanics operate within regulatory frameworks that vary by jurisdiction.[Qualified custodians](https://www.bitgo.com/products/qualified-custody/) answer to banking and trust regulators, with obligations around capital reserves, segregation of client assets and anti-money laundering law compliance. We’ll examine regulatory frameworks in more detail further in the article.


## Custody Models: Self, Hybrid, and Full


Some companies may be uncomfortable handing over complete control over their crypto asset wallets. For this reason, some crypto custody providers offer hybrid models with varying levels of access shared between the asset manager and the crypto custodian.


Custody Model


What It Means


Risk Management


Asset Manager Custody


The asset manager maintains full custody of the cryptocurrency assets.


The investment manager may be liable for losses.


Hybrid Custody


Custodians and financial institutions share multiple keys required for withdrawals.


Liability is shared between the custodian and financial institution.


Full Custody


Regulated custodian fully manages access.


Digital assets fully safeguarded by custodian, certain custodians, such as BitGo, maintain insurance policies that can help to reduce losses, subject to policy coverage specifics..


When asset security is the primary concern, full custody is the safest arrangement. With full custody, the experienced custodian is responsible for protecting the private keys used to withdraw cryptocurrency from a digital wallet.


If the financial institution wants a level of control, it can leverage the benefits of multi-signature wallets, which should be used almost always for institutional-level cryptocurrency management, regardless of the custody arrangement. For example, the asset manager could require a three-signature wallet, with two signatures needed to access the contents. The custodian and institution each hold one or two of the three keys, ensuring redundancy if a key is lost or compromised.


However, if the institution retains control over the cryptocurrency, all of the security and insurance benefits from the crypto custody provider may not apply. It's important to weigh the pros and cons of each potential custody model before moving forward.


## What to Look for in an Institutional Crypto Custody Provider


No two platforms are the same.

### Security Architecture


Institutional investors have teams spread across roles and geographic locations who may be interacting with any given asset. These security precautions balance convenient access for authorized individuals with preventing unauthorized withdrawals.


-


Offline[Cold Storage](https://www.bitgo.com/products/cold-wallets/) : It's normal to facilitate the speed of day-to-day trading by keeping some assets in online "hot" wallets. However, most funds should be stored in offline[cold wallets](https://www.bitgo.com/resources/blog/protecting-your-digital-assets-cold-wallets-and-their-benefits/) , which help protect against cyber threats by storing private keys on hardware disconnected from the internet.


-


Hardware Security Modules (HSMs): These hardware devices are designed to store sensitive cryptographic keys. Random numbers generate keys in secure, offline environments, and access is granted only to authorized personnel or quorums. As an added security measure, they're also designed to be tamper-evident, leaving a clear trail of access.


-


Multi-Site Storage: Requiring private keys to be accessed and authorized in varied geographic locations creates redundancy if sites are unavailable, and it provides security by separating authorization across multiple locations.


-


Multi-Signature Approval: By requiring multiple parties to approve transactions, a single point of failure is far less likely to compromise security.


-


Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA): Authenticator apps further reduce single points of failure by generating one-time codes authorized parties need in order to sign transactions.


When implemented effectively, these controls reduce the risk of private key exposure, whether through hacking, physical theft, or operational failure.


### Insurance Coverage and Fund Segregation


Custodians understand that insurance is essential for institutional investors to feel comfortable entering the digital asset space. No matter how many security measures are in place, something can always go wrong.


In some cases,[institutional-grade insurance](https://www.bitgo.com/resources/blog/the-importance-of-crypto-custody-insurance) will cover[cold storage](https://www.bitgo.com/products/cold-wallets/) and criminal acts. However, broader policies may also cover scenarios involving key management, theft, internal collusion, or administrative errors and omissions.


For reference,[BitGo maintains up to $250 million](https://www.bitgo.com/solutions/insurance/) in coverage for loss, theft, and misuse when BitGo holds all the keys.


Fund segregation is another critical safeguard. Some custodians operate affiliated trading platforms, which can introduce risk if legal entities or asset flows are not clearly separated. Segregated funds mean client assets are isolated in the event the custodian's trading platform goes bankrupt.


### Regulatory Standing and Qualified Custodian Status


[Qualified custodian](https://www.bitgo.com/products/qualified-custody/) status under the Investment Advisers Act of 1940 is essential for investment advisors.


But even though the distinction carries critical compliance consequences, some custody providers are not[qualified custodians](https://www.bitgo.com/products/qualified-custody/) , and may not be suitable to serve organizations with fiduciary obligations.


For institutions operating across borders, working with an institutional digital asset custody partner with regulatory standing across jurisdictions is critical. A custodian’s licensing and oversight should align with the regulatory requirements of every market an institution may serve.


Additionally, SOC 1 Type II and SOC 2 Type II certifications offer independent verification of a custodian’s security and financial controls. Such audits provide peace of mind knowing custodial controls are well-designed and operate effectively to protect client assets.


### Asset Support and Coin Coverage


While bitcoin is the best-known and most widely supported cryptocurrency, institutions may be interested in any number of other coins. Broad asset coverage enables portfolio flexibility without compromising compliance.


Institutions should confirm support for specific chains and tokens relevant to their strategy before selecting a provider.

[Integrated Trading](https://www.bitgo.com/products/trading/) APIs: Direct integration with brokers, exchanges, and internal systems is critical for scale. Institutions need to be able to trade both digital and traditional assets without managing cumbersome parallel systems.

Asset Coverage: With hundreds of coins on the market, broad coin coverage enables portfolio flexibility without compromising compliance standards.

[Off-Exchange Settlement](https://www.bitgo.com/products/go-network-oes/) and Over-the-Counter (OTC) Trades: Sizable trades on exchanges can move markets and negatively impact strike prices. Custodians that facilitate off-exchange trading tend to offer more competitive prices.


-


[Staking](https://www.bitgo.com/resources/blog/what-is-staking-crypto/) : Some coins use proof-of-stake consensus mechanisms to validate transactions and secure their network. For institutional investors, that means they can earn rewards in exchange for locking funds for a period of time.


-


[Integrated Trading](https://www.bitgo.com/products/trading/) APIs: Direct integration with brokers, exchanges, and internal systems is critical for scale.


### Reporting, Audits, and Compliance Infrastructure


Real-time trade reconciliation, comprehensive audit logs, and full transaction trails should be included as a standard, not a premium add-on. The right institutional cryptocurrency custody partner will understand no institution can enter the digital asset space without these systems in place.


Institutions should also expect custodians to hold SOC 2 certifications. While not required by law, any custodian who doesn’t submit themselves to regular and rigorous third-party audits should be regarded with caution.


Before selecting a provider, confirm both audit frequency and scope. Less strenuous SOC certifications test an organization’s controls at a specific point in time, but more rigorous variations ensure those controls operate effectively over an extended period of time (usually 6 to 12 months).


### Pricing Models and Total Cost


Avoid the temptation to choose a provider based on cost. Discount providers may have less robust security or inadequate insurance, among other drawbacks; the downside risk far outweighs any potential savings.


Institutional grade asset custody pricing models vary from provider to provider; evaluating the true cost of service across flat fees, variable fees, percent of assets under management, and hybrid models can be a challenge.


Rather than evaluating partners on cost, institutions should search for those who provide transparent, easy-to-understand pricing information, and confirm full fee schedules before committing.


## The Regulatory Landscape for Institutional Crypto Custody


Regulatory uncertainty has been perhaps the most significant hurdle preventing institutional adoption. Regulators failed to clearly define whether cryptocurrencies were commodities or securities, leaving institutions uncertain which regulator and which rules they were obligated to comply with.


However, that’s changing.


In the United States, the SEC[recently released materials,](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/capital-raising-building-blocks/crypto-assets-federal-securities-laws) describing how federal securities laws may apply to crypto assets, helps market participants better understand the Commission’s current views..


The SEC Custody Rule under the Investment Advisers Act requires[RIAs](https://www.bitgo.com/products/wealth-management/) with custody of client funds or securities to hold assets with a[qualified custodian](https://www.bitgo.com/products/qualified-custody/) ; a statement[issued in September 2025](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/peirce-statement-custody-crypto-assets-093025) by an SEC Commissioner discusses how[regulated entities](https://www.bitgo.com/entities/) may use state-chartered trust companies as[qualified custodians](https://www.bitgo.com/products/qualified-custody/) for crypto assets.


State regulators, such as the New York Department of Financial Services, have taken advantage of this permission to license certain organizations to provide[qualified custody](https://www.bitgo.com/products/qualified-custody/) services for digital assets.


Furthermore, the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC)[recently affirmed](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/news-releases/2025/nr-occ-2025-42.html) that, in addition to state-chartered trust companies, national[banks](https://www.bitgo.com/solutions/banks/) may custody digital assets on behalf of clients. BitGo[recently secured](https://www.bitgo.com/resources/blog/bitgo-secures-occ-approval/) an OCC national trust bank charter, operating as BitGo Bank & Trust, National Association.


The European Union, the United Arab Emirates (UAE), and other countries are also developing regulatory frameworks. Notably, know-your-customer (KYC) and SOC audits are becoming more common across jurisdictions.

Under Rule 206(4)-2, known as the Custody Rule,[RIAs](https://www.bitgo.com/products/wealth-management/) with custody of client funds or securities must hold those assets with a[qualified custodian](https://www.bitgo.com/products/qualified-custody/) . This entity is generally a bank, savings association, or trust company that meets specific regulatory standards.


Core requirements include client asset segregation, submitting to independent audits, and adhering to certain reporting requirements.[Qualified custodians](https://www.bitgo.com/products/qualified-custody/) specialized in digital assets will take additional precautions to safeguard client assets, including[cold storage](https://www.bitgo.com/products/cold-wallets/) for significant holdings, multi-signature authorization for transaction approval, and more.


## Comparing Institutional Crypto Custody Providers


CIO’s conducting due diligence for[institutional crypto custody](https://www.bitgo.com/solutions/investors/) providers have a challenging task ahead of them. They’re responsible for a decision that’s hard to undo, easy to second-guess, and carries significant risk-management implications for their organization.


At a high level, we recommend placing security and regulatory concerns well above cost concerns.


Stakeholders running RFPs for institutional-grade crypto custody providers might consider reading our[essential guide to picking digital asset custody providers](https://www.bitgo.com/resources/blog/essential-guide-to-picking-digital-asset-custody-providers/) . However, in brief, consider asking the following during a due diligence process:


-


What is their security incident history? Cybersecurity threats are especially dangerous in digital asset markets.


-


How are private keys managed? The differences between self, hybrid, and full custodial key management carry important security and liability implications.


-


Are assets insured, and under what circumstances? No matter the precautions, something can always go wrong. Ask who the underwriter is, what’s covered relative to AUM, and what scenarios are excluded from insurance coverage.


-


How often are they audited? Securing an SOC certification is no easy task, but maintaining it over time is even more difficult. Just because a custodian passed an audit in the past doesn’t mean they’re still maintaining those standards.


-


Are the service level agreement (SLA) terms clear? Any number of conversations may happen between a discovery call and a decision, but only the terms outlined in the SLA are legally binding.


-


What are the fee structures? Pricing models vary by provider, and may range from fixed and variable fees to assets under management-based pricing and hybrids.


In addition to questions one might ask of a prospective partner, it’s important to independently verify regulatory licenses, SOC certifications, insurance carrier, and policy limits.

## FAQs


### What is institutional crypto custody?


[Institutional crypto custody](https://www.bitgo.com/solutions/investors/) refers to third-party services that securely hold digital assets on behalf of professional investors. These providers offer enterprise-grade infrastructure, regulatory compliance, and risk management tools that go beyond what's available to retail investors on regular exchanges.


### How is institutional custody different from retail custody?


Institutional custody operates under regulatory frameworks, such as the Investment Advisers Act Custody Rule, which requires fiduciaries to take certain steps to safeguard client assets.


Conversely, retail custody typically operates with no fiduciary duty to the asset owner. Security and recovery are largely the individual’s own responsibility.


### What is a qualified custodian?


[Qualified custodians](https://www.bitgo.com/products/qualified-custody/) are legal entities that meet the[qualified custodian](https://www.bitgo.com/products/qualified-custody/) definition under Rule 206(4)-2 of the Investment Advisors Act, permitting them to hold client funds and securities on behalf of an RIA.


To meet the definition, they’re obligated to hold client assets separately from their own, undergo regular independent audits, and adhere to certain reporting requirements.


### How do institutional custodians secure digital assets?


Institutional custodians secure digital assets with advanced security measures such as[cold storage](https://www.bitgo.com/products/cold-wallets/) , multi-signature wallets, hardware security modules (HSMs), and multi-factor authentication (MFA). Such safeguards protect private keys and prevent unauthorized access.


Many also undergo independent audits, maintain insurance coverage, and design systems with geographic and operational redundancies to eliminate single points of failure.


### What features should I look for in a custody provider?


Find a provider that understands a financial institution's unique needs. For instance, multiple employees serving different functions (from compliance to trading and operations) may need access to a particular crypto key.


Does that provider offer a security protocol that accounts for those users while also providing timely access to[cold storage](https://www.bitgo.com/products/cold-wallets/) assets?


### Why do institutions need dedicated custody?


Institutions face higher fiduciary, legal, and operational standards than retail investors. The right provider helps meet those standards by reducing counterparty risk, complying with regulations, and ensuring assets are held securely, are auditable, and readily accessible.


### What are the risks of institutional custody?


While institutional custody significantly reduces risk compared with exchange custody, it still carries the risk of custodian insolvency, internal breaches, and human error. However, these risks can be mitigated by choosing a regulated, audited, and insured custodian with a proven track record.


### How should a CFO compare in-house vs. qualified custodian costs?


Add up the full cost of doing it yourself, including tools, people, around-the-clock monitoring, and the cost if something goes wrong. Compare that to the custodian's fees and any capital requirements, and factor in the value of freeing your internal team to focus on core business priorities.


### How do I run an RFP for a custody provider?


Decide what really matters to you up front, like security, ease of use, asset coverage, and quality of support, then turn that into a simple checklist and scoring sheet. Invite a small group of finalists to demo their platform with your team and push them for real examples of how they handled past problems, not just sales talk.


### What red flags should I watch for?


Be cautious if the firm is secretive about who owns it, how it holds client assets, or how it handles outages and hacks. Also, treat big "too good to be true" yield promises, heavy reliance on one key vendor, or unclear conflicts with their trading or lending activities as warning signs.
