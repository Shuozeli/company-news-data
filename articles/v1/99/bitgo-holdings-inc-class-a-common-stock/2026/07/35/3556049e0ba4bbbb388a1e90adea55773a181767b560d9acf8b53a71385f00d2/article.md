# Stablecoin Adoption: Why Businesses Are Shifting to Stablecoins for Faster Payments

## Key Takeaways


-


Stablecoin adoption is accelerating because traditional payment rails often struggle with slow settlement speeds, high transaction costs, and cross-border complexity.


-


Common use cases include cross-border payments, treasury and liquidity management between entities, and settlement in digital asset markets.


-


Stablecoin implementation necessitates systems-level integrations across finance, operations, and compliance teams encompassing API integrations, wallet infrastructure, key management, qualified custody, asset security, and record-keeping mechanisms.


-


Regulatory clarity is improving through frameworks like Europe’s MiCA and the US GENIUS Act, but requirements will continue to vary from jurisdiction to jurisdiction.


-


Stablecoins are unlikely to replace traditional payment systems. Their strongest role is filling gaps where legacy infrastructure underperforms, particularly in enabling fast, low-cost, cross-border transactions.


How long do you typically wait for a cross-border payment to settle?


If you’re measuring in days, you’ll understand why the stablecoin market cap[more than doubled](https://en.macromicro.me/charts/134292/world-stablecoin-market-cap) in the last two years; speed.


Traditional payment systems weren’t designed for the pace, geography, or complexity of modern business operations. Institutions adopting stablecoins aren’t speculating, nor are they replacing existing payment rails.


Rather, they’re supplementing with digital asset infrastructure that offers instant settlement times, built-in transparency, and lower transaction fees.


Institutions need to understand why stablecoin adoption is accelerating, business use cases, how enterprises implement in practice, and the operational requirements that determine whether adoption succeeds at scale.


## Why Stablecoin Adoption Is Accelerating


Stablecoins are digital assets designed to maintain value relative to a given fiat currency. They address frictions finance teams have contended with for years: slow settlements, high transaction fees, and the operational challenge of moving money across borders.


-


**Slow Settlement:** Transfers depend on correspondent bank networks that operate within fixed hours and geographic constraints, often taking up to five business days to complete.


-


**Transaction Fees:** Costs accumulate as funds travel across intermediary layers, reducing capital efficiency for high-frequency or low-margin transactions.


-


**Cross-border Complexity:** Transfers into developed countries can be slow or expensive, but in some developing nations, access to international banking infrastructure is so limited, certain transfers are impractical or effectively unavailable through traditional means.


The[benefits of stablecoins](https://www.bitgo.com/resources/blog/benefits-of-stablecoins/) complement, rather than replace, existing financial infrastructure. Certain transfers are best handled by traditional systems, while international transfers are sometimes better suited to the instant, low-cost, and intermediary-free transactions available through blockchain networks.


Growing regulatory clarity is also contributing to institutional stablecoin adoption


In addition to emerging global regulatory frameworks, the 2025[GENIUS Act](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2025/07/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-signs-genius-act-into-law/) created the first-ever Federal US regulatory system governing stablecoins; intended to ensure stability and trust, the law requires 1:1 reserve backing with liquid assets for all stablecoin issuers.


The Act takes effect on the earlier of January 18, 2027, or 120 days after final implementing regulations are issued.


## Stablecoin Use Cases for Business Operations


Cross-border contractor payouts, vendor invoices, and treasury rebalancing between entities are high-opportunity stablecoin use cases for businesses.


Digital asset infrastructure carries four key advantages over traditional financial systems:


-


**Cross-border Payments:** Traditional rails involve multiple intermediaries, currency conversion layers, and settlement windows that can span several business days.[Stablecoin rails for banks](https://www.bitgo.com/resources/blog/why-banks-are-upgrading-to-stablecoin-rails/) address this friction by settling transactions on-chain without corresponding intermediary dependencies. Transfers reach counterparties across jurisdictions within minutes, at lower cost, and with built-in transparency.


-


**Treasury and Liquidity Management:** Financial operations spanning multiple jurisdictions need liquidity across business entities, currencies, and time zones. On-chain balances can be monitored and reconciled in real time, improving visibility into cash positions across a distributed treasury structure. This represents a meaningful operational improvement over an often fragmented fiat banking infrastructure.


-


**Settlement in Digital Asset Markets:** Stablecoins provide a stable unit of account for executing trades, settling positions, and managing collateral in digital asset markets. They’re the primary settlement instrument for centralized exchanges, DeFi protocols, and cross-border digital asset liquidity markets.


-


**Deposits and Financial Services:** As regulatory clarity grows, so too does enterprise stablecoin adoption. Financial institutions and fintech platforms are increasingly[accepting stablecoin deposits](https://www.bitgo.com/resources/blog/accepting-stablecoin-deposits/) to take advantage of on-chain deposit-like functions, programmable payment workflows, and real-time account management.


## What Enterprise Stablecoin Adoption Looks Like in Practice


Picture this.


You have a manufacturing supplier in Singapore. They sent their invoice, net-30 terms, but the wire you sent Thursday still hasn’t cleared. It’s sitting somewhere in the correspondent banking chain, delayed by a public holiday nobody on the team flagged.


The payment is now three days late.


Your vendor relationship will be fine, but delivery will be delayed. They were relying on that cash flow to purchase raw materials.


Problems like these are key motivators behind enterprise stablecoin adoption.


Once you’ve defined a use case, the next challenge is integrating digital wallet infrastructure with existing treasury management systems.


Finance teams don’t want parallel fiat and digital asset payment systems. They need API integrations enabling them to initiate payment within the same interface they’re accustomed to using.


From here, the difference is that for a subset of counterparties, instructions route through wallet infrastructure rather than the SWIFT network. Settlement completes in real-time, rather than in a few days.


But before a single stablecoin transaction goes live, compliance needs to sign off on the governance framework. Which entities will you pay this way? Receive payment from? Under which jurisdictions? With what documentation requirements? And which approval workflows?


Once legal reviews the arrangement, finance needs a reconciliation workflow that maps on-chain transactions to accounting and bookkeeping workflows. Even stablecoins need cost basis tracking. Despite the 1:1 fiat peg, the value relative to USD fluctuates slightly over time, potentially leading to tax implications.


Coordination is the final challenge to solve.


Finance owns the reconciliation model and reporting obligations. Operations own the payment workflow and counterparty management. Compliance owns the policy framework and screening logic. And successful stablecoin adoption means integrating, on a systems-level, across all three.


## Infrastructure Required for Stablecoin Adoption


The benefit of stablecoin adoption depends entirely on the underlying infrastructure. If stablecoin implementation creates more inefficiencies than it solves, it’s not worth pursuing in the first place.

-


**API Integrations:** Infrastructure without robust API connectivity creates gaps and redundancies that undermine the value of stablecoin adoption. Traditional and stablecoin payment infrastructure needs to be connected. One payment interface, connected to the same compliant bookkeeping and accounting workflows your organization is already familiar with.


-


**Wallet Infrastructure and Key Management:** Institutional adoption necessitates institutional-grade security measures: multi-signature authorization, role-based access controls, balanced cold vs hot wallet storage infrastructure, and policy-enforced transaction limits.

**Blockchain Connectivity:** Up-time is crucial. Institutions interacting with blockchain networks need real-time transaction monitoring, with redundancy sufficient to support operational continuity.

## Operational and Regulatory Considerations


According to an[Ernst & Young survey](https://www.ey.com/en_us/insights/financial-services/cost-savings-and-speed-drive-stablecoin-adoption) , over half of financial institutions expect to implement stablecoins into their workflows in the near future. However, those same institutions overwhelmingly (73%) cite regulatory uncertainty as the top barrier to further adoption.


Luckily, regulatory certainty is coming into focus: Singapore, the UAE, Japan, and the EU already have comprehensive stablecoin regulations in place.


Europe’s MiCA legislation,[fully in force in December 2024](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-gl/industries/banking-capital-markets/documents/ey-gl-global-stablecoin-regulation-comparison-09-2025.pdf) , regulates stablecoins as “asset-referenced tokens” or “electronic money tokens.” For issuers, that means mandatory licensing requirements, 1:1 reserve backing, immediate redemption rights, and a ban on yield-bearing mechanisms.


But as regulatory requirements continue to develop, they’ll inevitably vary from jurisdiction to jurisdiction.


Institutions operating cross-border stablecoin workflows must assess how issuance, custody, and transfer activity are regulated in each market. Aligning operational considerations, from auditability to internal controls and approval workflows, with variable regulatory standards will be challenging.


Following regulatory uncertainty, Ernst & Young noted accounting (38%) and operational complexity (32%) as the most significant barriers to institutional adoption. Even if regulatory certainty disappeared tomorrow, integrating traditional financial systems that weren’t designed for digital asset ecosystems represents a significant challenge.


Treasury platforms, accounting systems, and compliance tools require custom API integrations that will take time and resources to build and maintain.


Digital asset infrastructure introduces new learning curves as well. The technology is there, but staff familiarity with governance frameworks and approval workflows, unique to on-chain processes, will take time to establish.


## The Future of Stablecoin Adoption in Institutional Finance


As of May 2026, the worldwide stablecoin market cap is[just ~$320 billion](https://en.macromicro.me/charts/134292/world-stablecoin-market-cap) . However, many[institutional stakeholders believe](https://www.ey.com/en_us/insights/financial-services/cost-savings-and-speed-drive-stablecoin-adoption) 5-10% of cross-border payments will be made using stablecoins by 2030, equating to trillions in annual transactions.


In short? Stablecoins are becoming a durable component of institutional financial infrastructure, complementing traditional payment rails with fast, inexpensive, cross-border transaction capacity.


Stablecoin adoption won’t replace existing systems. Rather, they’ll operate alongside them, filling gaps where existing infrastructure underperforms.

## FAQs


### How can stablecoins improve treasury operations, cross-border payments, or liquidity management?


Stablecoins help finance teams move value across borders more quickly and with fewer fees. They also provide real-time transaction visibility, reduce reliance on third-party banks, and make it easier to monitor on-chain balances across entities, jurisdictions, and time zones.


### What is the lowest-risk first use case for an enterprise adopting stablecoins?


This depends on the specific frictions your organization is facing.


However, cross-border supplier payments likely represent the safest entry point for many institutions. The use case is well-defined, the inefficiency being addressed is measurable, and the counterparty set is manageable.


Starting with a single payment corridor, rather than a broad rollout, limits complexity while producing operational data that informs whether and how to expand.


### What custody, compliance, and operational controls are needed before launching a stablecoin program?


Institutions need secure wallet infrastructure, role-based permissions and approval workflows, transaction limits, and qualified custody where applicable to fiduciaries.


Compliance teams also need KYC, AML frameworks in place, jurisdiction-specific regulatory reviews, and audit-ready reconciliation workflows.


### Should we start by accepting existing stablecoins or issue our own branded stablecoin?


Issuing a branded stablecoin is a meaningful step up in complexity and regulatory commitment.


For most institutions, adopting existing fiat-backed stablecoins, particularly those with established reserve attestations, regulatory standing, and broad network liquidity, is the appropriate starting point.
