## What Is a Tennis Bracelet?


A tennis bracelet is a flexible bracelet made up of a continuous line of individually set gemstones, typically diamonds, linked together in a symmetrical pattern. Each stone matches the others in size, shape, and quality, giving the bracelet its signature uniformity and consistent sparkle.


While the overall silhouette is classic, the design can vary in many ways. The gemstone type, setting style, and metal each offer room for personalization and all influence how the bracelet looks and what it costs.


## How to Choose a Tennis Bracelet


Buying a tennis bracelet involves several design decisions. Each one shapes the overall look, durability, and price of the piece.


### 1. Set Your Budget


Tennis bracelet prices vary widely, and your budget helps define which options are worth considering. Gemstone type, total carat weight, metal, and construction quality all drive cost.


Setting a clear budget at the start helps you focus on designs that fit your preferences without feeling overwhelmed. It also makes it easier to weigh tradeoffs, such as prioritizing stone size over quality, or choosing a more durable metal at a lower carat weight.


### 2. Choose the Gemstone


The gemstone is one of the most important decisions because it defines both the look and value of the bracelet.


**Type**


[Diamonds](https://www.brilliantearth.com/diamond/buying-guide/) are the most popular choice for their durability and versatility. They pair well with every metal type and hold up well to regular wear.


For something more distinctive,[colored gemstones](https://www.brilliantearth.com/gemstones/buying-guide/) are a strong alternative.[Sapphires](https://www.brilliantearth.com/gemstones/buying-guide/sapphire/) offer a range of rich hues and are durable enough for everyday use.[Emeralds](https://www.brilliantearth.com/gemstones/buying-guide/emerald/) provide a deep green tone but require more careful handling. Each gemstone brings a different character to the design.


**Color**


For diamonds, many buyers prefer colorless stones for a bright, uniform appearance, though[fancy color diamonds](https://www.brilliantearth.com/diamond/buying-guide/what-are-colored-diamonds/) are also available. For colored gemstones, the right choice comes down to personal preference and how the hue fits your overall style.


**Origin**


Natural gemstones come from the earth, while[lab-grown stones develop in controlled environments](https://www.brilliantearth.com/news/how-are-lab-grown-diamonds-made/) . Both share the same physical and visual properties. The difference mainly comes down to[price](https://www.brilliantearth.com/news/lab-grown-diamonds-price/) and sourcing preference.


### 3. Select Carat Weight


In a tennis bracelet, carat weight refers to the[total carat weight (CTTW)](https://www.brilliantearth.com/diamond/buying-guide/ct-ctw-cttw-tw-comparison/) of all stones combined. As total carat weight increases, the individual stones appear larger and more visually prominent, producing a wider bracelet with more presence on the wrist. Lower carat weights produce a finer, more delicate look.


Carat weight is often where buyers balance visual impact against budget, so it helps to decide early how bold or understated you want the bracelet to feel.


### 4. Choose the Design Details


With the gemstone and carat weight in place, the remaining design details shape the character and wearability of the bracelet.


**Stone Shape**


[Round brilliant stones](https://www.brilliantearth.com/gemstones/round/) are the most common choice because they maximize light reflection and produce consistent sparkle across the bracelet.[Princess](https://www.brilliantearth.com/gemstones/princess/) ,[oval](https://www.brilliantearth.com/gemstones/oval/) ,[cushion](https://www.brilliantearth.com/gemstones/cushion/) , and[emerald cuts](https://www.brilliantearth.com/gemstones/emerald-cut/) each create a different effect, ranging from sharper geometry to softer or more[elongated shapes](https://www.brilliantearth.com/diamond/buying-guide/shape/elongated-diamond-shape/) .


**Setting Style**


Prong settings use small metal tines to hold each stone, maximizing light exposure and enhancing brilliance. Bezel settings wrap the stone in metal, offering greater protection and a cleaner, more modern profile, though they allow less light into the stone.


**Metal Type**


[White gold](https://www.brilliantearth.com/jewelry/bracelet/tennis/white-gold/) and[platinum](https://www.brilliantearth.com/jewelry/bracelet/tennis/platinum/) provide a bright, neutral backdrop that keeps the focus on the stones.[Yellow gold](https://www.brilliantearth.com/jewelry/bracelet/tennis/yellow-gold/) and[rose gold](https://www.brilliantearth.com/jewelry/bracelet/tennis/rose-gold/) contrast with the stones, offering a distinct look with a romantic, vintage feel.


**Clasp**


A secure clasp matters for everyday and special occasion wear. Many tennis bracelets use a box clasp with a safety latch. Double clasp mechanisms add an extra level of security and are worth considering if the bracelet will see frequent use.


**Coverage**


Full coverage bracelets feature stones around the entire circumference of the wrist, creating a continuous line of sparkle. Half coverage styles place stones only along the top portion, which can improve comfort and reduce the overall cost.


### 5. Find the Right Fit


A well-fitting tennis bracelet should fit comfortably around the wrist, not too tight or too loose. Most bracelets range from 6 to 7.5 inches in length.


A good general guideline is to leave enough room to fit one finger between the bracelet and your wrist for the perfect balance of comfort and security. This allows the bracelet to have slight movement but not slip off the hand. If possible,[visit a jeweler](https://www.brilliantearth.com/stores/) to have your wrist measured before purchasing.

## FAQ


### Why is it called a tennis bracelet?


The term "tennis bracelet" became popular in the 1980s, thanks to professional tennis player Chris Evert. During a match in the 1987 U.S. Open, Evert's


[diamond bracelet](https://www.brilliantearth.com/jewelry/bracelet/diamond/) broke and fell off her wrist. She asked the match officials to pause the game until the bracelet was found. The incident was widely publicized, and from then on, the style became known as the "tennis bracelet." Evert's mishap highlighted both the bracelet's beauty and its durability, cementing its place in jewelry history.


### How much is a tennis bracelet?


Tennis bracelet prices vary widely. Prices generally start around a thousand dollars and scale upward based on the gemstone, total carat weight, metal, and setting quality.


### How should a tennis bracelet fit?


A tennis bracelet should sit close to the wrist without feeling too tight. A general guideline is to leave enough room to fit one finger comfortably between the bracelet and your wrist. This gives the bracelet room to move naturally without sliding past the wrist or bunching when you bend your wrist.


### Can you resize a tennis bracelet?


Yes, most tennis bracelets can be resized. A jeweler can typically add or remove links to adjust the length. The ease of resizing depends on the setting style and construction, so not every bracelet is equally straightforward to alter. If you are unsure of your size or buying as a


[gift](https://www.brilliantearth.com/gifts/) , ask a jeweler about resizing options before purchasing.


### Are tennis bracelets unisex?


Yes, tennis bracelets are unisex. Many styles are designed to suit a wide range of preferences, making them a versatile option for all genders.


### How do you open a tennis bracelet?


Most tennis bracelets are designed with secure clasps to keep them safely in place during wear. Opening the bracelet depends on the type of clasp used.


- **Box clasp with safety latch:** This is the most common style for tennis bracelets. Start by releasing the safety latch on the side of the clasp. Once the latch is open, gently press the sides of the box clasp and slide it apart to release the bracelet.


- **Lobster clasp:** Lobster clasps are less common but still used in some designs. To open one, press down on the small lever to open the clasp, then slide the bracelet free.


Because tennis bracelets are typically made with fine components, it helps to open and close the clasp carefully to avoid unnecessary strain on the mechanism.


### How do you clean a tennis bracelet?


Regular cleaning helps maintain the brightness and overall appearance of a tennis bracelet, especially if it is worn often. Over time, lotions, oils, and everyday residue can build up around the stones and setting.


A simple cleaning routine is usually enough for regular maintenance. Soak the bracelet in a solution of warm water and mild dish soap for a few minutes. Then use a soft-bristled toothbrush to gently clean around the stones and settings, where buildup tends to collect. Rinse thoroughly with clean water and dry with a soft, lint-free cloth.


For a deeper clean, a jeweler can use specialized tools to safely clean hard-to-reach areas and inspect the bracelet for any loose stones or wear. Occasional professional cleaning can help maintain both the appearance and longevity of the piece.
