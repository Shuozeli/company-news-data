## What Is a Diamond Alternative?


A diamond alternative is any gemstone or material used in place of a natural diamond, typically in[engagement rings](https://www.brilliantearth.com/engagement-rings/shop-all/) or[fine jewelry](https://www.brilliantearth.com/jewelry/shop-all/) .


Couples choose diamond alternatives for a variety of reasons: to reduce cost, to get a larger or more eye-catching stone, or simply because they prefer the look of a different gem. With a variety of alternatives available, the right choice depends on what priorities matter most to you.

## Best Diamond Alternative


Lab grown diamonds are widely considered the best overall diamond alternative. However, lab grown diamonds actually share the same physical, chemical, and optical properties as natural diamonds, so calling them an "alternative" is a bit of a misnomer. The only difference is their origin:[lab grown diamonds are created in controlled environments](https://www.brilliantearth.com/news/how-are-lab-grown-diamonds-made/) , while natural diamonds form deep within the Earth over millions of years.


But if we're comparing alternative options to a traditional mined diamond, lab grown diamonds are the closest match. They're available in the same range of cuts, colors, clarities, and carat weights, and they can be set in any[engagement ring style](https://www.brilliantearth.com/engagement-rings/buying-guide/styles/) .


For couples who love the look of a diamond but want a more accessible price point, lab grown diamonds deliver without compromise.

## Most Realistic Diamond Alternative


Lab grown diamonds are the most realistic alternative to natural diamonds because they are, chemically, optically, and physically the same material.


Even trained gemologists cannot distinguish a lab grown diamond from a natural one without specialized equipment. They exhibit the same brilliance, fire, and scintillation, and they're graded on the same


[4 Cs scale](https://www.brilliantearth.com/diamond/buying-guide/) ([cut](https://www.brilliantearth.com/diamond/buying-guide/cut/) ,[color](https://www.brilliantearth.com/diamond/buying-guide/color/) ,[clarity](https://www.brilliantearth.com/diamond/buying-guide/clarity/) , and[carat weight](https://www.brilliantearth.com/diamond/buying-guide/carat/) ).

## Top 5 Diamond Alternatives


Now that we've covered the standout picks by category, here's a closer look at the five most popular diamond alternatives and what sets each one apart.


### 1. Lab Grown Diamond


Lab grown diamonds are created in controlled laboratory environments using advanced technology that replicates the conditions under which natural diamonds form. The result is a stone with identical physical, chemical, and optical properties to a mined diamond.


Because of their wider availability, lab grown diamonds are offered at a lower price point than natural diamonds of comparable quality.


They're available in the full range of[shapes](https://www.brilliantearth.com/diamond/buying-guide/shape/) ,[sizes](https://www.brilliantearth.com/diamond/buying-guide/mm-to-carat/) , and quality grades, and they can be set in any ring style. For couples who want a diamond at a[lower cost than a natural stone](https://www.brilliantearth.com/news/lab-grown-diamonds-price/) , lab grown diamonds are the most direct option.


[Shop Lab Diamonds](https://www.brilliantearth.com/lab-grown-diamonds/)


### 2. Moissanite


Moissanite is a lab-created gemstone composed of silicon carbide, known for its brilliance and fire. It was first discovered in a meteorite crater in 1893, but because natural moissanite is extremely rare, virtually all moissanite used in jewelry today is lab grown.


With a hardness of 9.25 on the Mohs scale, moissanite is second only to diamond in durability, making it suitable for everyday wear in engagement rings. It's also more affordable than diamond, which allows for the ability to select larger stones or more intricate settings within the same budget.


One thing to keep in mind: moissanite displays more than twice the amount of fire than diamond, and this "disco ball" effect becomes especially noticeable in larger stones. Some love this rainbow sparkle, while others prefer the more subdued brilliance of a diamond. It comes down to personal preference.


[Shop Moissanites](https://www.brilliantearth.com/gemstones/moissanite/)


### 3. White Sapphire


White sapphire is a colorless variety of corundum, the same mineral family as blue sapphire and ruby. With a hardness of 9 on the Mohs scale, it's one of the more durable gemstones available and a suitable option for daily wear.


It offers a clean look with a softer, more understated sparkle than diamond or moissanite. Because it reflects light more subtly, it appeals to those who prefer a muted appearance over bold brilliance, while also coming in at a lower price point that can allow for a larger stone.


[Shop Sapphires](https://www.brilliantearth.com/gemstones/sapphire/)


### 4. White Topaz


White topaz is a colorless gemstone that offers a bright, clean appearance at a very accessible price. With a hardness of 8 on the Mohs scale, it is durable enough for everyday wear, though not as hard as sapphire, moissanite, or diamond.


It exhibits a luster, meaning it has a smooth, glass-like shine when cut and polished. Compared to diamond or moissanite, its sparkle is more subtle, creating a softer, understated look.


For those who prefer a simple, refined aesthetic and are looking for an affordable colorless stone, white topaz is a practical option.


[Shop Topaz](https://www.brilliantearth.com/gemstones/topaz/)


### 5. Cubic Zirconia


Cubic zirconia (CZ) is a[diamond simulant](https://www.brilliantearth.com/gemstones/buying-guide/simulated-diamond/) made from zirconium dioxide. At a glance, it can resemble a diamond, though the differences become more noticeable upon closer inspection.


CZ is highly transparent and displays a pronounced rainbow-like effect. Because it lacks the natural inclusions found in most diamonds, it can appear almost too perfect, making it easier to distinguish to the untrained eye.


CZ is more prone to scratching, chipping, and losing its luster over time. It has a hardness of 8 to 8.5 on the Mohs scale but is more porous, which means it can cloud or become dull with prolonged use, especially when exposed to chemicals or everyday wear.


CZ is the most affordable option on this list. A 1-carat stone may cost only a few dollars, compared to hundreds or thousands for a diamond, moissanite, sapphire, or topaz. This makes it a practical choice for temporary settings, travel rings, or pieces that are worn occasionally.


For an engagement ring intended for daily wear over many years, other alternatives in this guide offer greater long-term durability.

## Why Choose a Diamond Alternative?


There are several practical reasons to consider a diamond alternative for your engagement ring.


- **Budget flexibility.** Diamond alternatives are typically more affordable than natural diamonds. Many alternatives allow you to get a larger or higher-quality stone for less, freeing up budget for the setting, wedding planning, or other financial priorities.
- **Variety and personalization.** Choosing a non-traditional center stone is one way to create a ring that reflects personal style. From the fire of moissanite to the understated look of white sapphire, each alternative has its own visual character.
- **Ethical considerations.** Lab grown options offer a more traceable sourcing path. For couples who value responsible sourcing, alternatives can provide added peace of mind.
- **Durability for everyday wear.** Several diamond alternatives rank high on the Mohs hardness scale, meaning they can handle daily wear with minimal risk of scratching.


The best choice comes down to your priorities. If matching the look of a diamond matters most, a lab grown diamond is your top contender. If budget is the driving factor, moissanite and white topaz stretch the furthest. And if durability and low maintenance are key, white sapphire is hard to beat.


## FAQ


### Is there an alternative to diamonds?


Yes, there are several alternatives to diamonds for engagement rings. The most popular options include lab grown diamonds, moissanite, white sapphire, white topaz, and cubic zirconia.


Each offers a different balance of appearance, durability, and price. Lab grown diamonds are the closest match to natural diamonds in every way, while moissanite, white sapphire, and white topaz offer their own distinct characteristics at lower price points.


The best alternative depends on what you value most, whether that's brilliance, budget, longevity, or a combination of all three.


### Is moissanite a good diamond alternative?


Moissanite is one of the more popular diamond alternatives. It ranks 9.25 on the Mohs hardness scale, making it scratch-resistant and suitable for daily wear. Its brilliance and fire exceed that of diamond, though the sparkle has a more rainbow-like quality compared to diamond's balance of white and colored light.


Moissanite can also be significantly more affordable than both natural and lab grown diamonds, which means you can typically get a larger stone for the same budget.


For couples who want a durable stone at a lower price point, moissanite is a popular choice.
