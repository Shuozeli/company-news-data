# Kenzo Perfumes: How a Japanese Fashion Designer Built a Global Fragrance House

Some of the best things come out of blending cultures. For example: Nikkei cuisine combines Japanese precision and bold Peruvian flavors to create something entirely unique. There are also Maison Margiela’s iconic Tabi shoes, which reference the traditional Japanese worker’s shoes. And,[if you’re somewhat of a fashion nerd](https://www.scentbird.com/blog/90s-perfumes-nostalgia/) , you’ll know that no conversation about beauty and fashion is complete without mentioning French fashion house Kenzo.


## Kenzo: the French fashion house that highlights Japanese craftsmanship


Founded in Paris in 1970 by Japanese fashion designer Kenzo Takada, the house is the perfect balance of Japanese craftsmanship and Parisian high fashion. It’s often instantly recognizable through its bold graphic prints featuring oriental elements, as well as its sophisticated silhouettes that showcase the best of Western tailoring. As Takada once said, “Fashion is like eating. You shouldn’t stick with the same menu.”


In 1993, the brand joined the LVMH group, expanding its global reach and refining its fragrance portfolio into the distinctive scents the house is known for today. The fragrances translate the house’s playful language into scent, making it easy to wear across different moods and moments.


##
A timeline of Kenzo perfumes


From the very first King Kong to L’Eau Pure launched just last year, these iconic Kenzo fragrances trace the evolution of the house’s bold identity.


### KENZO King Kong (1978)


The very first Kenzo perfume was 1978’s King Kong, which, if you think about, goes with the brand’s original name, Jungle Jap. Taking inspiration from Takada’s many travels, the now-discontinued fragrance was spicy and oriental, featuring banana ([a note that is making waves again in 2026!](https://www.scentbird.com/blog/banana-matcha-marshmallow-perfume-gourmand-fragrances/) ), resin, oak moss, and amber.


### [KENZO Flower](https://www.scentbird.com/perfume/kenzo-flower-by-kenzo-151364327146568) (2000)


Arguably the most famous of Kenzo’s *parfums* , Flower is often described as clear. Inspired by the naturally scentless poppy flower, this unique fragrance blends pink pepper, sensual damascena rose, vanilla, and white musk to create a timeless blend that teases the nose. Its cylindrical, slightly tilted bottle has also become instantly recognizable, almost like a modern design object in its own right.


### [KENZO L’Eau Pure](https://www.scentbird.com/perfume/kenzo-l-eau-pure-276138172492076) (2025)


Crisp and slightly citrusy, the unisex L’Eau Pure was inspired by Takada’s poetic vision: “Water has no color. So, I wanted a water bursting with color, with fruits, flowers, freshness, and happiness.” To do this, the fragrance was created with the purity of Aquozone® and white lavender, which was then blended with lemon, mandarin, Australian sandalwood and musk.
