# Why MSPs Should Bet on Microsoft 365 Workspace Management for AI Readiness

AI readiness starts with strong data foundations and operational discipline, which enable organizations to adopt AI confidently, responsibly, and in line with evolving regulations. According to[AvePoint-commissioned research from Omdia](https://www.avepoint.com/ebooks/ai-opportunity-governance-msp-research) , 51% of MSPs say data governance and compliance challenges are the main obstacle to AI adoption. Without consistent governance, fragmented environments make it harder to control access, maintain compliance, and scale AI initiatives securely. Effective workspace management reduces compliance risk by automating provisioning, enforcing sensitivity labels, and operationalizing data lifecycle controls.


With[75% of SMBs](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/smbs-ai-trends-2025/) experimenting with AI, managed service providers (MSPs) have a significant opportunity to become a driver of AI readiness strategy for organizations.[Omdia forecasts](https://omdia.tech.informa.com/blogs/2025/sep/from-generative-ai-to-agentic-ai-the-us-276-billion-partner-opportunity) that by 2030, the global partner opportunity for AI services will reach US$267 billion, given that governance and compliance remain the most frequently cited barriers to AI adoption. Responding to these AI barriers, MSPs can implement workspace management to ensure AI-enabled workflows and business processes are implemented responsibly and transparently, in alignment with regulatory requirements.


To meet this demand, MSPs must invest in multitenant visibility, standardized baselines, and integrated automation. These are the core capabilities that enable MSPs to operationalize governance as a scalable, recurring service — rather than just a cost of doing business.

## Building a Workspace Management Practice for MSPs


Simply put, organizations increasingly expect their partners to run operations seamlessly so internal IT teams can focus on high-value tasks and strategic initiatives — from infrastructure modernization to strengthening cybersecurity defenses. MSPs can meet this need by offering[structured, well-governed workspace management practices](https://www.avepoint.com/products/elements/workspace-management) .


Here’s how to build a scalable practice:


- **Assess client needs.** This first step involves understanding clients’ current setup, identifying gaps in their management, and determining their readiness for AI integration. This is crucial, as over[87% of SMBs](https://smallbiztrends.com/small-business-cybersecurity/) collect or process sensitive customer data that could be compromised, underscoring the need for strong governance to prevent breaches and support regulatory compliance.
- **Develop a comprehensive strategy.** Once the needs are assessed, MSPs should develop a comprehensive strategy that includes policies, procedures, and tools for effective workspace management. This strategy should align with the client‘s business goals and regulatory requirements.
- **Implement baseline management tools.** MSPs should implement[tools that can streamline tenant onboarding](https://www.avepoint.com/products/elements/baseline-management) by automating provisioning, applying secure configuration templates, and enforcing governance policies from the start. This approach reduces manual setup, accelerates time-to-value, and transforms data migration into recurring service opportunities.
- **Train and support.** To assist with a successful practice, MSPs should offer training sessions to help clients use the tools effectively and provide continuous support to address any issues that arise.
- **Monitor and optimize.** MSPs should continuously monitor the client’s workspace to ensure management tools function as intended and compliance is maintained. Regular audits and automation of data classification help track data lineage and enforce compliance policies.
