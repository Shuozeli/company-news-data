# How an energy abundance mindset can give America a competitive edge


3 min read | March 20, 2026


## The United States is the world’s largest producer of oil and natural gas and a leading producer of five other key energy resources.


Chevron Chairman and CEO Mike Wirth discussed America’s energy advantages and an “all of the above” energy approach on the *Ruthless Podcast* .


During a March 12 appearance on the *Ruthless Podcast* , Chevron Chairman and CEO Mike Wirth said America’s energy abundance should be developed to support economic growth, U.S. competitiveness and national security.


## Key facts that frame energy solutions


- The United States ranks No. 1 globally in oil, natural gas, natural gas liquids, liquefied natural gas, biofuels and nuclear energy production.
- Oil and gas demand remains strong, challenging common decline narratives.
- Electricity demand is surging due to AI and digital infrastructure.
- Energy security equals national security, according to Wirth.
- An abundance mindset means building more supply, more infrastructure and more optionality.


Wirth says that the United States can benefit from developing its abundance of energy resources.


## The U.S. is positioned to meet rising demand for energy


Oil and gas demand is strong and continues to grow worldwide. Wirth noted that this is particularly true in developing economies that are industrializing and improving living standards.

## AI and data centers are driving an increasing need for power


The rapid growth of AI and digital infrastructure is also creating a surge in electricity needs. Existing grids and renewable buildouts cannot meet that demand in the near term. Wirth presented this as a looming challenge that policymakers and the public are not fully accounting for.


Wirth said that permitting and development processes are constraining the ability to provide the electricity needed to power new technologies like AI.


## America’s abundance of resources


The United States can develop its vast pool of energy resources as an economic benefit. Wirth stressed that progress requires sequencing—scaling new solutions while maintaining existing systems. He added that one perfect solution is unlikely, and that this is the privilege of having an abundance of energy resources in the country.
