# Driven by People: Jessica Novich’s Leadership Journey at Full Tilt

By Plumas Bank


May 19, 2026


[Business banking](https://www.plumasbank.com/blog/category/business-banking)


At[Full Tilt Logistics](https://fulltiltlogistics.com/) , one core concept is woven into every workday: a culture of connection.


Chief Operating Officer Jessica Novich works alongside her husband, sister, brother-in-law and mother-in-law. Employees consistently refer friends to join the company. Some team members have gotten married after meeting there. Everyone shares the same break room.


For Novich, that closeness — both in proximity and relationships — is anything but accidental. In fact, it’s part of the culture she has worked intentionally to create and protect as the business has expanded.


“We like to have that whole family feel throughout the organization,” she said. “We work hard, but we play hard. We have fun.”


It’s a philosophy that has helped guide Full Tilt’s growth from a six-person startup into a thriving Reno logistics company — and one of the many reasons Plumas Bank recently nominated Novich as a Nevada Women’s Fund Woman of Achievement. The honor celebrates women who make significant contributions to the Northern Nevada community through leadership, service and professional excellence. And for those who know her best, the recognition feels fitting.


“Jessica has been instrumental in the growth and development of the Full Tilt brand the past 12 years,” said Teresa Courrier, senior vice president and commercial loan officer at Plumas Bank. “Originally established as a woman-owned logistics business in 2014, product lines now include transportation, warehousing and touring divisions.”


Courrier also points to something equally important: the personality behind the title.


“Not only is she a super businesswoman, she’s quick with a laugh when you ask her about her kids, Cris and the new chicken coop,” she said.


The unique combination of operational leadership and genuine warmth shows up consistently in the way Novich talks about her work, her team and her community.


## Competitive Roots


Born and raised in Reno, Novich graduated from the University of Nevada, Reno, where she studied communications and business while playing collegiate softball — an experience she says still shapes her approach to leadership today.


> “You take a lot of accountability in sports,” she said. “You learn how to work with a team. You learn how to lead a team. You’re all working together to achieve a common goal. So it all kind of ties in together through my athletic career, but crosses over into my professional career as well.”


That connection to athletics still plays a major role in how Novich gives back to the community today. Full Tilt serves as a presenting sponsor for University of Nevada women’s athletics, an investment she describes as deeply personal.


“It was a huge impact in my life playing for the university and having the ability to be coached and develop leadership during that era of my life,” she said. Supporting the next generation of female student-athletes, she adds, feels like an opportunity to help create those same experiences for others.


She credits her sporting background with teaching her how to collaborate and stay level-headed under pressure, skills that became invaluable as Full Tilt evolved from a small startup into a rapidly growing company.


“When I first joined, we were in a tiny office out in Spanish Springs, and there were probably six of us,” she recalled.


Today, Full Tilt employs approximately 68 people across its brokerage, transportation, warehousing and distribution operations. Along the way, Novich has experienced several moments when she witnessed the company truly scaling.


## Early Tipping Points


One came, she noted, the first time she went truck shopping.


“To actually look at the trucks and see the value of them — and wrap your mind around not only the liability, but the expenses that come with that side of it — that was a big moment,” she remembered.


Trucks came to symbolize not only her company’s growth, but also a fundamental truth about life that Novich thinks people take for granted: “Pretty much everything you see around you is moved by a truck,” she reflected. “It’s a big part of how our world operates. I don’t know if a lot of people think about that. They’re what drive everything.”


Another moment of scalability for Full Tilt came when the company acquired warehouse space.


“To go in there and see all that vacant space, knowing that we were going to rework the racking and fill it to fit our customers’ needs, was pretty awesome,” she said.


Even as Full Tilt expanded, Novich said the company worked intentionally to preserve its culture. That emphasis on people sits at the center of her leadership philosophy.


“It’s a hill that I will die on,” Novich said. “Our team is what makes Full Tilt, Full Tilt. If you take care of your team, your team feels valued and appreciated, and that flows into your customers. Without them, we would be nothing.”


Over time, she also learned an important lesson many entrepreneurs struggle to embrace: to trust others enough to let go of complete control. This was an essential lesson considering Novich oversees elements of many diverse aspects of the business, including finance, cash management, HR, administration and marketing.


But she notes that developing trust was a process and wasn’t necessarily an instinctive impulse. Novich admits her early mindset used to be more along the lines of, “I’ll just do it myself.” But eventually, she realized sustainable growth required something different.


## Building Trust & Celebrating Women


> “If you want to have a balance between your family life and your work life, you need to start trusting people,” she said. “I wanted to take the time to teach my team how to do these things, and then let them take on those responsibilities and really let them fly.”


That investment in her team has helped create space for a healthier balance between leadership and family life — something she deeply values during this season of raising children ages 11, 9 and 4 alongside her husband and business partner.


Novich also recognizes the unique perspective women bring to leadership roles within logistics, an industry where women remain underrepresented.


“I will say that while we’re still the minority, there are more women than you would think, especially in decision-making roles,” she said. “I think women are very good at multitasking and navigating through solutions and thinking quickly on our feet.”


## Relationships Matter


While a connection to people remains at the center of Novich’s business, this core concept also influenced Full Tilt’s longstanding relationship with Plumas Bank.


“We wanted somebody local that we could have a relationship with,” she said. “They’ve helped us navigate decisions throughout our growth, and they’ve really been a huge and wonderful partner for us.”


Relationships with employees, clients, family, banking partners and their community guide Full Tilt’s trajectory, she notes. And even as the company continues to evolve, leadership remains deeply connected to the values that grounded the business from the beginning. Those priorities show up in the company’s culture, its support of Northern Nevada organizations and athletics and the way Novich talks about success itself — less as a finish line and more as the ability to create opportunities for others.


“This is our home,” she said. “I want to see businesses and people succeed in this area. Being able to provide jobs and opportunities here is really important.”
