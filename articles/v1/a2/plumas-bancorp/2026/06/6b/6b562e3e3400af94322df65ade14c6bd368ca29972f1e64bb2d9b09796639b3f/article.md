# The Coach Approach: ICBA 40 Under 40 Honoree Matt Baca Talks Teamwork, Accountability & Trust

By Plumas Bank


June 1, 2026


[Community banking](https://www.plumasbank.com/blog/category/community-banking)


Matt Baca was a mere 4 years old when he climbed onto a table at one of his dad’s company Christmas parties, commandeering the room to deliver a brief but heartfelt speech thanking everyone for attending.


His parents still laugh about what became his very first public address. In fact, they even gave him a nickname because of it: “Matt the Diplomat.”


Decades later, the title still fits. And it perhaps tells part of the story about why Baca, senior vice president and commercial lending manager at Plumas Bank, was recently recognized by the[Independent Community Bankers of America (ICBA) as one of its 40 Under 40 Community Bank Leaders for 2026](https://www.icba.org/w/40-under-40-2026-s-community-bank-leaders) .


The national award — which recognizes “community banking’s brightest and most promising young professionals” — honors emerging leaders making meaningful contributions to community banking and the communities they serve. Candidates are judged on character, leadership, community involvement and innovation.


For Baca, the recognition took a while to sink in.


“I didn’t realize the full magnitude of it at first,” he said. “I didn’t really think I would get something like this. Once it settled in, I was pretty ecstatic.”


The distinction acknowledges many of the qualities Baca’s family noticed early on — comfort connecting with people, curiosity about what drives them and a genuine interest in helping others succeed.


“I love seeing people grow and develop,” he said. “And to play a small role in their success is an honor.”


## Leadership Built on Trust


A worldview dedicated to helping others shapes Baca’s approach to community banking. He works with business owners throughout the Plumas Bank footprint, helping guide conversations around expansion, commercial real estate, long-term planning and lending strategy. He describes the strongest banker-client relationships as honest partnerships built over time.


“You’re helping them grow and cultivate their business,” he said. “Sometimes you’re having tough conversations. You’re not always telling them what they want in the moment, but you’re helping set them up for success in the long term. And you’re standing by them to celebrate those successes when they happen.”


He believes these conversations work best when trust exists on both sides.


> “There has to be enough trust to really peel back the layers together and have honest discussions,” he said. “I’ve had many clients thank me for my perspective, but I tell them they’re doing the hard work — I’m just here to help them out a little bit.”


Baca has honed his trust-building skills over a career dedicated to banking. After graduating from UNLV with degrees in management and finance, Baca moved to Los Angeles and joined a large commercial bank. The financial industry was a solid fit for him because of the problem-solving aspect of the work.


“I figured out that I love resolving issues that others aren’t able to figure out,” he said.


Over time, he found himself drawn to the tenets of community banking.


“I was attracted to how community banks really look at the full scope of every transaction — from the borrower’s perspective, the impact on the community and just doing what's right. And that's what drove me here to Plumas Bank,” Baca recalled. “We don't put anybody in a credit box. We look at every single client and every single transaction as a standalone and make sure that the client, the bank and our shareholders all benefit.”


Now eight years into his Plumas Bank career, Baca oversees lenders across Northern Nevada, northeastern California and into southern Oregon while continuing to maintain close relationships with clients and colleagues alike.


## Lessons from the Field


A lifelong soccer player, Baca now coaches his 9-year-old daughter’s club soccer team. The sport has become a clear metaphor for his leadership strategy.


“When it’s time to train and develop the team, that’s when you’re asking the questions and implementing ideas,” he said. “But when it rolls over to game time, as their coach, I should be silent. That’s their time to show what they’ve got, and it’s my time to watch and see what they’ve learned.”


He applies the same philosophy at work.


> “If I’m micromanaging and telling everybody what to do all the time, then I’m not doing my job right,” he said. “I should be able to stand back a bit and let them flourish and adapt. The only way people can grow is if you stay out of their way, see how they navigate challenges and get them out of their comfort zones.”


The mindset traces back to lessons from his father, who worked in logistics and gave Baca a list called “The Baca’s 10 Rules of Business” after he graduated college. He still keeps the printed-out list in his home office.


“One of the top rules was, ‘Make the call that nobody wants to make and take the call that nobody wants to take,’” Baca said. “That always stuck with me. You know, have the uncomfortable conversations and lead by example. I've been more successful in working with team members and clients by just having the conversations.”


## Protecting Home Base


Outside the bank, Baca and his wife, Heaven, stay busy raising their two daughters, Milania and Faith. The family spends much of its free time outdoors — hiking, camping, playing soccer and exploring the Tahoe area with their two malamutes.


The couple also serves as a foster family. They fostered Faith when she was just 1, finalizing her adoption last year when she was 2.


“There are so many little ones who need a home, need a family, need love,” Baca said. “We've been so blessed to see Faith go from not being able to walk to cruising all over the house and talking, laughing, and she’s always just so, so excited to see her big sister.”


He also volunteers, donating time regularly to the Food Bank of Northern Nevada, Project Marilyn, and Junior Achievement financial literacy programs to introduce foundational money concepts to elementary-age students. He serves on the Finance Committee for the Nevada Discovery Museum and is treasurer for CREW of Northern Nevada.


## Watching Others Win


Even with national recognition arriving in the middle of a busy season of work and family life, Baca still sounds slightly uncomfortable discussing his own accomplishments.


“I’ve never been very good at receiving praise,” he shared with a laugh. “Everybody else is really excited about ICBA, which is fun to see. It still hasn’t fully hit me yet.”


Despite receiving this national recognition, he still talks far more comfortably about others’ accomplishments than his own.


> “I measure my success by other people’s success,” he said. “As a coach or as a banker — when my team does well, we all do well. And I get to just be a small part of it, which is really fulfilling.”


Baca says he takes time to reflect on his role routinely, and in fact, recalls a recent conversation with longtime Plumas Bank ag lender John Rayl — who retired in May after 35 years — about the importance of relationships in banking.


During his retirement celebration, Rayl shared with Baca that he’s sad to step back from meaningful relationships that were both personal and professional. He said he still sees clients around town, and they mention that he helped them purchase their first ranch or overcome a genuinely hard time.


“That conversation really resonated with me because it reminded me we’re all working toward the same thing,” Baca said. “It's about the clients. It's about the relationships. It's about the community impact. And I realized, we aren't just out here because it's a job. It's something that we really enjoy, and we’re here to make a difference for others and for our communities.”


Overall, Baca exhibits the kind of subtle yet impactful leadership that doesn't require him to shout from the rooftops — or even stand on a table to command attention. The nickname “Matt the Diplomat” may have started as a family joke, yet it still reflects the way Baca approaches both banking and life — listening closely, building trust through connection and ensuring his team feels supported along the way.
