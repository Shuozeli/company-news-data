# Deal Insights Dashboard: What Sales Leaders Actually Use (vs What They Ignore)

Sales leaders check three things on a deal insights dashboard: at-risk deals this quarter, rep-level activity against target, and forecast accuracy. Everything else — call sentiment scores, talk-to-listen ratios, competitor mention frequency, keyword trends — gets built, shipped, and ignored.[67.7% of business leaders say they use dashboards to monitor sales performance](https://improvado.io/blog/sales-dashboard) , up from 54% in 2023. But[only 34% of organizations are highly confident in their deal insights](https://salesmotion.io/blog/deal-insights-that-close-revenue) . The gap between “we have a dashboard” and “it changes what we do” is where most sales tech budget disappears.


This post breaks down what gets used, what gets ignored, and why the next generation of deal insights is moving from dashboards to actions taken on behalf of the rep.


## The three things sales leaders check


Ask any VP Sales what they open on Monday morning and you'll get a version of the same list.


**1. At-risk deals in the current quarter.** Not every deal at risk — just the ones big enough to move the number. A VP running a EUR 4M quarter cares about the six-figure deals slipping, not the EUR 8k SMB that went quiet. Which named deals moved backwards this week, and why.


**2. Rep-level activity against target.** Meetings booked, calls made, pipeline created, pipeline closed. This is the coaching-and-accountability layer. It answers the question the VP has to answer to their CRO: is this a team problem or a person problem?


**3. Forecast accuracy over time.** Not this quarter's forecast — the delta between what reps committed 30 days ago and what closed. This is the trust metric. It tells the leader whether their pipeline conversations are reality-based or wishful.


Three things. Everything else on the dashboard is either input to one of these three, or it's noise the leader has trained themselves to scroll past.


## What gets built and ignored


Modern conversation intelligence platforms ship dashboards with 20 to 40 widgets. Here's what sales leaders skip:


- **Talk-to-listen ratios.** Interesting once. Actionable never. A leader isn't going to open a dashboard, see rep X talked 62% of the time, and do anything about it during business hours.
- **Sentiment scores.** “This call had negative sentiment” is not a next action. The rep already knew.
- **Keyword and competitor mention trends.** Useful for marketing and product. Not for a sales leader trying to hit a number this quarter.
- **Call volume heat maps.** Pretty. Uncorrelated with revenue.
- **Team-wide “top objections this month” clouds.** Good for a QBR slide. Never checked between QBRs.
- **Custom-property fill rates.**[Dataparrot's own product update](https://dataparrot.ai/blog/product-update-won-deal-analysis-march-2025) leads with fill-rate analytics as a feature. Sales leaders care about fill rates when their CRM is on fire — which is most of the time, but the dashboard doesn't fix it.


The pattern is clear. Sales leaders ignore anything that doesn't map to a named deal or a named rep, and anything that doesn't tell them what to do next. They don't need more visibility. They need fewer, sharper decisions surfaced at the moment they can act on them.


## Why “more metrics” is the wrong direction


The default take in the category is that a better dashboard has more data.[FreJun's 2025 sales statistics roundup](https://frejun.com/sales-statistics/) points out that 35–50% of deals go to the vendor who responds first, and 45–50% of B2B deals need 5–8 follow-ups. The insight isn't hidden. The problem isn't visibility. Watching a dashboard doesn't respond, follow up, or update the CRM. A rep does. Or an AI agent does.


This is where conversation intelligence stops short. It records the call, transcribes it, flags the deal as at-risk, and shows it on a dashboard. Then it waits for a human to open the dashboard, notice the flag, decide what to do, and do it. Most of the time — managers spend less than 5% of their time coaching, and reps spend less than 40% of their week selling — nothing happens.


The dashboard is a report. The report assumes someone will act on it. In practice, the report is the ceiling of what the tool does, not the floor.


## What the next generation of deal insights looks like


The shift is from *what happened* to *what to do about it, and did anyone do it* . Three things drive that shift:


- **Deal risk surfaces where the rep works.** In the CRM, in the calendar, in the follow-up drafting queue — not on a separate dashboard the rep visits once a quarter.
- **The action is drafted, not recommended.** A follow-up email drafted and waiting for review. A CRM field pre-filled and waiting for approval. A next-step suggestion in the deal record with the reasoning attached.
- **The dashboard is a byproduct of adoption, not the reason for it.** If reps use the tool because it saves them 30 minutes per call and drafts their follow-ups, the leadership visibility comes for free. If the tool exists only for the leader, no one uses it and the data goes stale within a quarter.


One Demodesk customer put it directly in a recent quarterly review: *“I like the rep actions part of it. That's good. That's a good addition.”* The dashboard wasn't the thing that landed. The actions taken on the rep's behalf were.

## Why dashboards fail in mid-market sales


Mid-market sales teams (100 to 5,000 employees) are structurally different from enterprise. The team is small enough that the VP knows every rep and every large deal by name. A dashboard that tells the VP something they already know — Sarah's champion went quiet on the Enpal deal — trains the VP to stop opening the tool.


What works instead: the tool notices the champion went quiet, drafts a re-engagement email in Sarah's voice, and puts it in her drafts folder with the context of the last three calls attached. Sarah reviews it, edits, sends. The VP finds out when the deal closes, not when it slips.


This is the operational shift conversation intelligence is going through in 2026. The[$2.9B revenue intelligence category](https://improvado.io/blog/sales-dashboard) grew on the promise of visibility. The next layer — the[AI sales platform](https://demodesk.com/ai-sales-agent) — grows on the promise of action. Visibility is table stakes. Action is the wedge.
