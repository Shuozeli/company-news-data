# The Employer’s Guide to Hiring Employees in Hawaii

## Step 3: Check Hawaii labor laws


Before you start writing[job descriptions](https://gusto.com/resources/hiring/job-descriptions) and corresponding with job candidates, take the time to review[federal labor laws](https://www.dol.gov/general/aboutdol/majorlaws) and[state labor laws](https://labor.hawaii.gov/wsd/wage-and-hour-faqs/) . These laws don’t just affect your employment practices and policies—they also play a role in how you recruit and interview prospective workers.


It’s critical to know Hawaii’s wage and hour requirements,[the difference between full-time employees and independent contractors](https://gusto.com/resources/articles/hr/team-management/independent-contractor-vs-employee#:~:text=An%20independent%20contractor%20is%20a,basis%20for%20a%20single%20employer.) , and Hawaii’s state laws around pay equity and equal opportunity.


Here are some common labor law topics from the Hawaii Department of Labor:


-


**Minimum wage:** The[Hawaii minimum wage](https://labor.hawaii.gov/wsd/minimum-wage/) is currently $16/hour. It will increase to $18/hour in 2028.


-


**Overtime:** Per federal law (under the Fair Labor Standards Act, or FLSA), non-exempt Hawaii employees who work more than 40 hours in a workweek must be paid overtime pay at a rate of 1.5 times. Here are more[Hawaii wage and hour laws](https://labor.hawaii.gov/wsd/wage-and-hour-faqs/) .


-


**Pay frequency:** Hawaii employers are required to[pay employees at least twice a month](https://labor.hawaii.gov/wsd/unpaid-wages/#frequency) on regularly scheduled paydays. Employees who are laid off or who quit must be paid the wages owed to them on the next regularly scheduled payday.


-


**Breaks at work:** Hawaii law mandates[rest or meal breaks](https://labor.hawaii.gov/wsd/breaks-meal-rest/#:~:text=In%20Hawaii%2C%20the%20only%20requirement,five%20consecutive%20hours%20of%20work.) for workers who are aged 14 or 15.


-


**At-will employment:** The[state of Hawaii is an at-will state](https://labor.hawaii.gov/wsd/illegal-termination-from-your-job/#:~:text=Generally%2C%20Hawaii%20is%20an%20%E2%80%9Cat,be%20notified%20of%20the%20reason.) . As an employer, you have the right to fire employees at any time without reason or cause. Your employees also have the right to leave their jobs at any time without reason or warning.


-


**Sick leave, jury duty leave, and vacation:** There’s no employment law in Hawaii that requires employers to[provide sick leave, jury duty leave, or paid vacation](https://labor.hawaii.gov/wsd/vacation-and-sick-leave/) . You get to choose what you offer your employees, but it’s worth noting that[offering employee benefits](https://gusto.com/resources/articles/benefits/why-employee-benefits-are-important) , like paid time off and healthcare, can help you attract better job candidates and improve employee happiness on the job.


-


**Domestic violence or sexual assault leave:** Hawaii employers with 50 or more employees are required to[provide up to 30 days of unpaid leave for employees (or their children) who’ve experienced domestic or sexual abuse](https://www.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrscurrent/vol07_ch0346-0398/HRS0378/HRS_0378-0072.htm) . Any employer with fewer than 50 employees is also required to provide unpaid leave, up to five days.


-


**Voting time leave:** Employers in Hawaii have to give employees[two hours of unpaid time off to vote during elections](https://elections.hawaii.gov/wp-content/uploads/2015/03/FS515VS001-Time-Off-for-Voting.pdf) (unless the employee works a 9–5 shift).


-


**Bone marrow and organ donation leave:** Employers are required to provide up to seven days of paid leave for[employees donating bone marrow](https://dhrd.hawaii.gov/state-employees/employee-benefits/family-friendly-benefits/bone-marroworgan-donor-leave/) and up to 30 days for organ donation.


-


**Salary history:** Hawaii employers cannot[ask job candidates about their salary history](https://www.mercer.com/en-us/insights/law-and-policy/salary-history-ban-now-the-law-in-hawaii/#:~:text=Hawaii%20employers%20with%20at%20least,1%2C%202019.) or use salary history to determine a new hire’s pay or benefits. See which[other states have salary history laws](https://gusto.com/resources/articles/hr/hiring/salary-history-ban-map) .


-


**Pay transparency:** In 2023,[Hawaii enacted a pay transparency law](https://www.shrm.org/resourcesandtools/legal-and-compliance/state-and-local-updates/pages/hawaii-enacts-pay-transparency-law.aspx) that requires Hawaii employers with at least 50 employees to disclose an hourly rate or salary range in job listings.


-


**Paid leave:** The[Hawaii Family Leave Law](https://labor.hawaii.gov/wsd/hawaii-family-leave/) applies to employers with 100 or more employees. Under this law, covered employers have to provide employees with up to four weeks of paid leave for qualifying events, like new parent bonding and caregiving.


-


**Discrimination laws:** Under the[Hawaii Employment Practices Act](https://www.hawaii.edu/uhwo/clear/home/HRS378.html#:~:text=For%20any%20employer%20to%20discriminate,%2C%20disability%2C%20or%20marital%20status.) , employers cannot discriminate against employees on the basis of race, sex (including gender identity or expression), sexual orientation, age, religion, color, ancestry, disability, or marital status.


-


**Pay equity:**[Hawaii’s Pay Equity Law](https://www.capitol.hawaii.gov/sessions/session2019/bills/HB1192_.HTM) makes it illegal for employers to pay an employee less than another employee of the opposite sex for equal work.


-


**Criminal history:**[Hawaii’s Ban the Box law](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title14/c090c/index.html) prohibits employers from using arrest history or court records as a basis for employment discrimination.

## Step 4: Fill out the Hawaii new hire reporting form


Part of the process of hiring new employees in Hawaii is completing new hire reporting.[New hire reporting](https://gusto.com/resources/articles/hr/team-management/new-hire-reporting) is a national system used primarily to locate parents who are late on their child support payments.


In Hawaii, you need to report new hires (part-time, full-time, and seasonal employees) to the[Hawaii Child Support Enforcement Agency](https://ag.hawaii.gov/csea/) within 20 days of the employee’s official date of hire. The hiring date is the first date an employee performs payable services or is able to earn a commission.


When reporting newly hired employees or rehired employees, you’ll provide your employee’s name, address, Social Security number, date of birth, and work start date, as well as your business information. There are two ways to report new hires in Hawaii:


1. You can fax or mail the employee’s HW-4 form to the address below:


Child Support Enforcement Agency


New Hire Reporting


Kakuhihewa Building


601 Kamokila Blvd., Suite 251


Kapolei, HI 96707


**Fax:** (808) 692 7001


2. Use the[new hire file format](https://ag.hawaii.gov/csea/files/2022/12/FLO101.pdf) to send your employee’s information.


For more information,[read these new hire reporting FAQs](https://ag.hawaii.gov/csea/files/2022/12/FLO100.pdf) .


## Step 5: Complete other hiring documents


After you report new hires, carve out some time to complete these[other key hiring documents](https://gusto.com/resources/articles/hr/team-management/new-hire-paperwork) (and make sure you save copies of everything for your records):


-


**Employment contract:**[Writing an employment contract](https://gusto.com/resources/articles/hr/hiring/do-i-need-to-use-an-employment-contract) for new employees keeps everyone on the same page. In the contract, explain your employee’s job responsibilities and expected pay, as well as your business’s[workplace policies](https://gusto.com/resources/articles/hr/team-management/what-include-employee-handbook) and employee handbook.


-


**Form I-9:** Form I-9, the Employment Eligibility Verification Form, verifies that employees are able to work in the US. As an employer, you have to complete the I-9 form for every employee you hire, and each employee has to attest to their employment authorization. You don’t have to file Form I-9 with the US Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) or Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE). Instead, just hold onto the form as a record for at least three years from the date of hire or from one year after employment ends. You can download Form I-9 and read the completion instructions[here](https://www.uscis.gov/i-9) .


-


**Form W-4:** Every new employee you hire needs to[complete IRS Form W-4](https://gusto.com/resources/articles/payroll/how-fill-out-w-4) , the Employee’s Withholding Certificate, on or before the date of their employment. Form W-4 determines how much federal income tax will be withheld from the employee’s paychecks.[Download the form](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/fw4.pdf) .


-


**Form HW-4:** You also need to give employees[Form HW-4](https://files.hawaii.gov/tax/forms/2022/hw4_i.pdf) , the Hawaii Employee’s Withholding Allowance and Status Certificate, to fill out.


## Step 6: Display labor law posters and required notices


The Hawaii DLIR requires employers to post labor law signs in the workplace that list basic employee rights and workplace obligations, like minimum wage standards and anti-discrimination policies.


Check out[Hawaii’s required labor law postings here](https://labor.hawaii.gov/labor-law-poster/) . After you download those, review the[required federal labor law posters](https://www.dol.gov/general/topics/posters) from the US Department of Labor.

## Frequently Asked Questions


### What are Hawaii's payroll and tax requirements for new hires?


When you hire new people in Hawaii, you have to give them Form HW-4, the Hawaii Employee’s Withholding Allowance and Status Certificate, to fill out. You have to pay Hawaii state income tax on their behalf, as well as state unemployment insurance.
