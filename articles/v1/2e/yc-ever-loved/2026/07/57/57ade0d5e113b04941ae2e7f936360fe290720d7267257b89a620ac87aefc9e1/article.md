### How to get started writing an obituary


Writing an obituary can feel like a big task, but breaking it down into steps can help you create a thoughtful tribute. Here’s a guide to get you started:


### 1. **Start with the basics**


- Include the full name of the person, any nicknames, and their age.
- Mention the date and place of their passing.


### 2. **Give background on their life**


- Share where they were born and raised, their education, career, and major life milestones.
- Include personal details that made them unique, like hobbies, passions, and favorite activities.


### 3. **Highlight their relationships**


- List family members (spouse, children, grandchildren, siblings) and close friends.
- If appropriate, mention predeceased family members, especially if they had a significant influence.


### 4. **Share their values and legacy**


- Describe what they were known for, what they believed in, or what made them special to the community.
- This could include achievements, volunteering, or how they impacted others' lives.


### 5. **Mention funeral or memorial details**


- If the obituary is going to be public, include information about funeral or memorial services.
- If donations are preferred in lieu of flowers, provide details about how people can contribute.


### 6. **Add a personal touch**


- Conclude with a meaningful sentence or two. This could be a favorite quote, a family saying, or a sentiment about their legacy.


### 7. **Edit and review**


- Read it over and share with a family member or close friend to ensure it captures the essence of your loved one.

## Standard obituaries


A standard obituary is a way to give an overview and story of someone’s life after they’ve passed away. Standard obituaries usually include all of the standard details of someone’s life (date of birth, death, those they’re survived by, etc.) but it also gives an overview of what they’ve accomplished and were passionate about in their life. People will often write about how they affected communities around them or other people that were close to them, how their families came to be, what was important to them in their free time, etc. If you’re looking for a more in depth overview of what to include in an obituary, please reference our article on[How to Write an Obituary.](https://everloved.com/articles/obituaries-and-funeral-announcements/how-to-write-an-obituary/)
