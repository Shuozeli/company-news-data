Losing someone close to you can be incredibly difficult, and if you’re responsible for handling funeral arrangements and personal affairs, the experience is often overwhelming. If this applies to you, here’s a death notification checklist of things that need to be taken care of after someone passes away. Note that some of these items can only be managed by the executor of a person's estate, so if this isn't you, it's generally a good idea to work closely with the person who is. This checklist is ideal for family survivors, for those looking for what to do after the death of a parent, or after the death of a spouse, step by step.


### Why use a checklist after the death of a parent, spouse or another loved one?


When someone experiences the death of a spouse or parent or another loved one, an after-death checklist can be incredibly helpful. After experiencing a death, most individuals can feel overwhelmed, emotionally exhausted, mentally exhausted, and even physically unable to perform basic tasks. Individuals are often suddenly faced with questions they've never even considered the answer to.


When you add in the grief, the practical affects of a death, the amount of tasks that need to be handled, and the need to often maintain a "normal" handle on personal responsibilities in life, it becomes extremely difficult for even the most knowledgeable and organized individuals to deal with. That's where a checklist can come into play as a useful resource. Not sure what to do after the death of a spouse? Parent? Military retiree? Ever Loved's death checklist is available as a PDF and as an always-online tool that you can access whenever you need.

### What to do as soon as possible


**Get a legal pronouncement of death.** If your loved one died in a hospital, a doctor can take care of this for you. However, if your loved one passed at home or in another location, you'll need to know who to call. If he or she passed away while in hospice care, call your hospice nurse. If your family member wasn’t at a hospital or in hospice, call 911.


**Arrange for organ donation, if applicable.** Check your loved one’s driver’s license and/or advance directive to see if he or she was an organ donor. If so, let hospital staff know immediately (or call a nearby hospital if your[loved one died at home](https://everloved.com/articles/post-death-logistics/things-to-do-with-a-deceased-persons-clothing/) ). Organ donation is time-sensitive, so this is one area where it’s important to act quickly.


**Notify close friends and family.** Every family is different, and there’s no one right way to do this. For some families, sharing the news in-person or over the phone is critical. For others an email or text message may be alright. If possible, split up the task between several family members.


**Decide what you’d like to do with your loved one’s body and arrange transportation.** First, check to see if your loved one expressed any wishes about final disposition or had made prepayments to a funeral home or[cemetery](https://everloved.com/cemeteries/) . Ideally, there will be documentation with other medical documents. If no wishes or plans have been stated, you have three main options:


-


*Call a funeral home.* A[funeral home](https://everloved.com/funeral-homes/) can help you arrange either a burial or cremation. We recommend checking reviews and prices for a few different funeral homes before making a decision, as both can vary widely. A few minutes of research can save you thousands of dollars and reduce unwanted surprises.


-


*Call a direct cremation company.* While you can arrange a[cremation](https://everloved.com/articles/funeral-planning/what-is-cremation/) through a funeral home, there are also cremation-specific companies that will work with you directly if you aren’t interested in the added services of a funeral director. A direct cremation through a cremation company can be one third of the cost of a direct cremation through a funeral home.


-


*Call a full-body donation organization.* Your loved one may have already registered to be a[body donor](https://everloved.com/articles/funeral-planning/how-to-donate-body-to-science/) , so check for paperwork. If he or she hasn’t, there are still many programs that accept donations from next of kin. Many[university medical programs](http://anatbd.acb.med.ufl.edu/usprograms/) rely on body donations, and other for-profit companies, such as[Science Care](http://www.sciencecare.com/) and[BioGift](http://www.biogift.org/) , will cover most costs and coordinate with other research programs. Body donation is often a good option for families who want their loved one to be able to continue to help others after death or who are looking for a more economical alternative to a traditional funeral.


[Use the checklist](https://everloved.com/after-death-checklist/)


**Arrange care for any pets or dependents.** If your loved one was responsible for caring for one or more people or pets, quickly find someone who can care for them temporarily while you figure out a long-term plan.


**Secure major property.** If your loved one lived on their own, make sure his or her home and any vehicles are locked up. If it will sit vacant for some time, consider notifying the landlord and/or the police, so they can help to keep an eye on it.


**Notify the person’s employer.** If the deceased was employed (or actively volunteering), call to let them know that your loved one has passed away. This is also a good time to ask about pay owed, benefits and life insurance.

### What to do within a few days


**Notify the broader community.**


**Research COVID-19 funeral restrictions and guidance for your area.** Laws and safety recommendations for[funerals during the COVID-19 pandemic](https://everloved.com/articles/funeral-planning/planning-funeral-during-covid19/) are constantly being updated, so it's a good idea to have a firm understand of what you should and shouldn't do before starting to put together funeral plans.


**Decide on funeral plans.** If you decided to work with a funeral home, get in touch with the funeral director to go through your options. (While COVID-19 is still prevalent, it's better to do this over the phone, instead of in person.) If you opted for an immediate burial (burial without a view or funeral beforehand), cremation or donation to science, you may also choose to hold a memorial service or celebration of life at a later date. Some people prefer this in order to give themselves more time to plan a meaningful event when they’ve had a little more time to process the death. It's also becoming more popular to postpone the service, so that a bigger gathering is possible once COVID-19 is less of a risk. **Create a memorial website.** A[memorial website](https://everloved.com/online-memorials/) will make it easy for you to share a death announcement and any funeral plans with a larger circle of people. This can become your primary place to share important information with everything.

**Notify the broader community. ** Inform friends, extended family, neighbors, and other relevant community members about the passing of your loved one. This can be done through personal calls, emails, social media posts, or an obituary in local newspapers to ensure that those who knew the deceased are informed of the situation. It can also be done by sharing an[online memorial website](https://everloved.com/online-memorials/) that you created.


**Start going through the mail.** Begin the process of sorting through the deceased person's mail to identify important documents, bills, subscriptions, or any outstanding matters that require attention. This step helps in managing the deceased's affairs and prevents important correspondence from being overlooked.

**Sign up for the DDNC list.** Register the deceased person's name and address with the Deceased Do Not Contact (DDNC) list to minimize receiving unwanted solicitations, advertisements, or marketing materials addressed to them after their passing. **Settle any trusts.** Work with legal and financial advisors to settle any trusts established by the deceased, ensuring that assets are distributed according to their wishes and in compliance with legal requirements. This may involve reviewing trust documents, identifying beneficiaries, and overseeing the transfer of assets as specified.


**Get additional support.** Seek emotional support from friends, family members, support groups, or mental health professionals to help cope with the loss and navigate the practical challenges of handling affairs after a loved one's death. Be prepared to spend a few hundred dollars if you'd like to publish it in the paper, but remember you can always[publish an obituary online](https://everloved.com/obituaries/) for free.

The eulogy may include personal anecdotes, memories, achievements, and reflections on the person's life, character, and impact on others.

### What to do within a few weeks


**Order a headstone.** Arrange for the design, production, and installation of a headstone or grave marker to commemorate the deceased's final resting place. This[permanent marker](https://everloved.com/funeral-products/headstones) serves as a tribute to their memory and provides a place for loved ones to visit and pay their respects.


[Browse headstones](https://everloved.com/funeral-products/headstones/)


**[Order several copies of the death certificate.](https://everloved.com/articles/post-death-logistics/how-get-death-certificate-every-state/)** You’ll likely need anywhere between 5 and 10 copies (but possibly more), depending on the accounts that your loved one had open. Your funeral director may be able to help you order them, or you can order them yourself from city hall or another local records office.


**Start the probate process with the will.** After death, searching through documents should be checked off your checklist. **Contact the Social Security office.** Your funeral director may have already done this, so find out if this is the case. If you need to contact social security yourself, you can reach them by phone at 1-800-772-1213. **Notify any banks or mortgage companies.** If you’re unsure of what accounts your loved one held, use their mail and any online accounts you have access to in order to identify what accounts may be open. You may need a court order to open and inventory a safe deposit box if a key isn’t readily available.


**Notify any car lenders.** Inform the relevant financial institutions or lenders about the death of the individual if they had outstanding car loans or leases. This step ensures that appropriate arrangements can be made regarding the vehicle, such as transferring ownership or settling any outstanding debts.


**Notify cable and internet provider(s).** Contact the cable and internet service provider(s) to notify them of the deceased's passing and to arrange for the cancellation or transfer of services as needed. This prevents unnecessary billing and helps manage the deceased person's accounts and obligations.


**Notify the cell service provider.** Inform the cell service provider about the death of the account holder to cancel or transfer the cell phone service plan and prevent any unauthorized usage or billing. This step helps manage the deceased person's accounts and responsibilities.

**Notify utility companies.**[Contact the relevant utility companies](https://everloved.com/articles/post-death-logistics/how-to-handle-utility-bills-after-someone-dies/) , such as electricity, water, gas, and garbage services, to notify them of the death and to make arrangements for the closure or transfer of utility accounts. This ensures that billing is properly handled and prevents unnecessary charges.


**Cancel online subscriptions.** Review and cancel any online subscriptions, memberships, or recurring payments associated with the deceased person's accounts to avoid ongoing charges and to manage their digital footprint. This may include streaming services, software subscriptions, or online publications.


**Cancel physical subscriptions.** Cancel any physical subscriptions, such as magazines, newspapers, or mail-order services, that were registered in the deceased person's name to prevent continued deliveries and charges. This helps manage their affairs and avoids unnecessary reminders of the loss.


**Reach out to any financial advisors or brokers.** Try to identify any additional financial and investment accounts that your loved one held. Work with each one to transfer ownership. You’ll likely need a death certificate for each account.

**Determine any employment benefits.** If your loved one was working at the time of their death, contact his or her employer to find out about union death benefits, pension plans and credit unions.

### Continue to help settle the estate


**Close other online accounts.** Identify and close any other online accounts registered in the deceased person's name, such as social media accounts, online shopping accounts, or gaming accounts. This helps manage their digital presence and prevents unauthorized access or use.


**Close any email accounts.**[Notify the email service provider](https://everloved.com/articles/post-death-logistics/how-to-close-email-accounts-after-someone-dies/) to close the deceased person's email account and deactivate it to prevent unauthorized access and protect their privacy. This step helps manage their digital assets and communications.


**Cancel and transfer money from online payment accounts.** Cancel any online payment accounts, such as PayPal, Venmo, or digital wallets, and transfer any remaining funds to a designated beneficiary or estate account. This ensures that the funds are properly managed and distributed according to the deceased person's wishes.

**Transfer unused airline miles and close accounts.** Contact the[airline loyalty program](https://everloved.com/articles/end-of-life-affairs/does-southwest-airlines-offer-a-discount-on-bereavement-flights/) to transfer any unused airline miles or rewards points to a designated beneficiary or to close the account if transfer is not possible. This ensures that the rewards are not lost and can be utilized by someone else.

**Notify any additional creditors.**[Inform any additional creditors](https://everloved.com/articles/post-death-logistics/how-to-notify-creditors-after-a-death/) or financial institutions of the deceased person's passing to settle outstanding debts, loans, or financial obligations. This ensures that creditors are notified and proper arrangements are made for repayment or resolution.


**Distribute items mentioned in the will.** Follow the instructions outlined in the deceased person's will regarding the distribution of specific items, assets, or possessions to beneficiaries or heirs. This ensures that their wishes are fulfilled and assets are distributed according to their estate plan.


**Sell any unwanted valuables.** Identify and sell any valuable items or possessions that are not designated for distribution in the will or desired by family members or beneficiaries. This can include jewelry, collectibles, or other assets that can be monetized to benefit the estate.

This allows loved ones to retain meaningful items and keepsakes as mementos of the deceased.


**Donate other useful items.** Donate remaining items, such as clothing, furniture, or household goods, to charitable organizations or community groups in need. This ensures that useful items are repurposed and benefits those less fortunate while honoring the legacy of the deceased.


**Throw away anything that's remaining.** Dispose of any remaining items, documents, or possessions that are no longer needed or have no sentimental or practical value. This helps streamline the process of managing the deceased person's estate and reduces clutter or unnecessary belongings.


### Miscellaneous items to take care of


**Send thank you notes.** Express gratitude to friends, family members, neighbors, and others who provided support, condolences, assistance, or contributions during the difficult time following the death of a loved one.[Sending thank you notes acknowledges](https://everloved.com/articles/funeral-etiquette/how-write-funeral-thank-you-note-cards/) their kindness and helps foster connections within the community.


**Consider future memorial events.** Reflect on the possibility of[organizing future memorial events](https://everloved.com/articles/funeral-planning/how-to-plan-an-informal-memorial-service/) , such as anniversary remembrances, tribute gatherings, or charitable activities in honor of the deceased. Planning for future memorials allows loved ones to continue celebrating and honoring the life and legacy of the departed individual.


**Create your will.** Take the opportunity to[create or update your own will](https://everloved.com/articles/end-of-life-affairs/how-to-create-will-online/) , outlining your wishes for the distribution of assets, guardianship of dependents, and other important decisions to be made in the event of your passing. Having a clear and legally-binding will helps ensure that your wishes are carried out and minimizes confusion or disputes among your beneficiaries.


**[Record your own information.](https://everloved.com/key-info/)** Compile and organize important personal information, documents, and instructions for your loved ones to access in the event of your death. This may include financial account details, passwords, insurance policies, medical directives, and other pertinent information to facilitate the handling of your affairs and estate.

**Consider life insurance.** Evaluate the benefits of obtaining[life insurance coverage](https://everloved.com/articles/end-of-life-affairs/how-does-life-insurance-work/) to provide financial protection and support for your loved ones in the event of your death. Life insurance can help cover expenses such as funeral costs, outstanding debts, mortgage payments, and ongoing living expenses, easing the financial burden on your family during a difficult time.


### Does all of this stuff really happen after someone dies?


No, not everything on this checklist is guaranteed to be applicable to you and your specific situation. However, when someone dies, a surprising number of tasks need to be handled. This entire process can be lengthy, especially when legal or financial complications arise, and it often takes an emotional toll on those closest to the deceased. Having a checklist handy is a great way to reduce the mental load and keep all of your tasks organized and in one place.


### Can I handle or plan for any of these tasks in advance?


Yes, many of these tasks can be planned for in advance, making the process easier for loved ones when the time comes. You can use the[key information checklist](https://everloved.com/key-info/) to start planning ahead of time which can significantly lighten the load after someone passes. Planning ahead is also an excellent way to make sure plans are in place and you're making decisions without dealing with the immense grief you may be feeling after the death of a loved one. By organizing these details in advance, you can give your loved ones a clear guide, allowing them to focus more on mourning and supporting each other.
