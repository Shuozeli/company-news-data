## What are Match Rates and What to Realistically Expect?


Match rate is the share of your anonymous visitors a tool can identify, and it's one of the first things to check before you buy.


**A red flag:** if a vendor claims 90% or higher match rates, ask them to walk you through the methodology. **** The highest realistic company-level match rates are 60-70%, and they require combining multiple data sources (IP, cookies, first-party data, third-party enrichment). ## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)


### How does website visitor identification software work?


Most of your website traffic is anonymous. Someone lands on your pricing page, reads two case studies, and leaves without filling out a form. Visitor identification software exists to put a name to that session.


Here's the chain, roughly in the order it happens.


When a visitor hits your site, the tool captures their IP address and browser signals. It matches that IP against a database of known company IP ranges, which is how you get the company behind the visit. This part is mature and reasonably accurate for businesses on their own networks.


Person-level reveal is the harder step, and fewer tools do it well.


It relies on a mix of deterministic and probabilistic matching. Deterministic means a hard identifier: a cookie tied to a past form fill, an email link click, a CRM record. Probabilistic means the tool infers the individual from behavioral patterns, LinkedIn data, and seniority signals.


Once a visitor is matched, the tool enriches the record. It appends firmographics like company size and funding, contact data like a verified email or phone, and sometimes technographics showing what software the company runs.


The last layer is intent. Good tools don't just tell you who visited; they weigh what the visit means. A pricing page view from a returning Tier 1 account scores higher than a one-off blog read. Some platforms stop at the reveal. Others push that scored lead straight into outreach.


That split, reveal-only versus reveal-plus-action, is the single biggest difference between the tools on this list.


### How to choose website visitor identification software


Before you look at logos, get clear on what you actually need the tool to do. Five questions do most of the sorting:


- **Do you need people or just companies?** Company-level reveal tells you "someone from Acme was here." Person-level tells you "Priya in RevOps was here, and she spent four minutes on pricing." If your motion is high-touch B2B sales, person-level changes what your reps can do. If you run broad account-based campaigns, company-level might be enough.
- **Where's your traffic?** US-built tools and EU-built tools have different strengths. If most of your visitors are in Europe, a GDPR-native tool like Leadfeeder or Leadinfo will out-match a US-first tool on that traffic, and vice versa.
- **Do you want to act inside the tool, or export to something else?** Some tools are a clean reveal engine and nothing more. That's fine if you already own a sequencer and a chat tool. If you'd rather not stitch four vendors together, look for platforms that fold intent scoring, chat, and outbound into one place.
- **What's your CRM?** Native HubSpot and Salesforce support is common. Pipedrive, Zoho, and Close often mean a Zapier workaround. Check this before you buy, because a broken CRM sync kills the whole workflow.
- **What match rate can you live with?** No tool identifies everyone. If a vendor promises near-total coverage, that's a reason to dig in, not relax. Which brings us to the next section.


### What identification rate should you realistically expect?


Match rate is the share of anonymous visitors a tool can put a name to. It's one of the first numbers to pressure-test before you sign anything.


A red flag worth naming: if a vendor claims 90% or higher, ask them to walk you through the methodology.


Realistic company-level match rates top out around 60 to 65 percent, and hitting that requires combining several data sources. Person-level is a different story. Even the tools built for it land in the 5 to 20 percent range, weighted toward 10 to 15 percent, and that's on B2B traffic in a good month.


A few things drag those numbers down, and they're mostly outside your control. Remote and hybrid work broke a lot of IP-to-company matching, since people browse from home networks. VPNs mask the signal entirely. Mobile traffic is harder to resolve than desktop. And smaller companies show up in fewer databases than the Fortune 500.


Warmly reports roughly 65 percent company-level and 15 percent person-level, using a waterfall across more than 20 data providers to widen coverage. Those are vendor-reported figures, so treat them as a starting benchmark and test against your own traffic during a trial, which is exactly what a free tier is good for.

### What visitor identification software can't do


No honest guide to this category skips the limits, so here they are.


You can only see visitors who land on your site. And person-level reveal carries privacy obligations that company-level doesn't, which is the next question most buyers ask.


### What's the difference between company-level and person-level identification?


Company-level tells you an organization visited: "someone from Acme was on your pricing page." It's mature and reasonably accurate, and it's what most tools in this category do.


Person-level tells you the individual: "Priya in RevOps was on your pricing page for four minutes." It's harder, fewer tools do it, and match rates are lower, but it's what lets a rep run a real one-to-one follow-up without guessing which of Acme's 400 employees to contact.


### Is website visitor identification GDPR compliant?


It can be, but compliance depends on the tool and on how you use it, not on the category itself.


Company-level identification generally carries lighter obligations, since a company IP isn't personal data in the same way an individual's details are. Person-level reveal involves personal data, which brings GDPR and CCPA obligations around lawful basis, transparency, and data-subject rights.


EU-built tools like Leadfeeder, Leadinfo, and Albacross lead with GDPR-native data practices. If you operate in Europe or handle EU visitors, confirm a vendor's specific compliance posture and check with your own legal team before rollout, since this isn't legal advice.

Budget a few days to a couple of weeks to get from "installed" to "running the way you want," depending on how much automation you're wiring up.
