# BR Partners: Feedback from the Non-Deal Roadshow

# BR Partners: Feedback from the Non-Deal Roadshow


BR Partners


Original article:[Access link](https://www.brpartners.com.br/en-US/home.html)


OCTOBER 08, 2024


5 minutes of reading


Today (08), we held a non-deal roadshow with BR Partners in Rio de Janeiro, where investors discussed the company's strategy and developments with Danilo Catarucci, Partner and Head of Capital Markets, and Vinicius Carmona, Partner and DRI. Despite the challenges in the Brazilian capital markets since its IPO in 2021, BR Partners has maintained solid profitability and expanded its revenue sources. This, combined with a strengthened balance sheet, increases its resilience and sustains a solid pace of growth. The dominant theme at the meetings was the current state of the capital markets. Overall, the discussions not only reinforced our positive view of the company, but also left us with the impression that the positive earnings momentum should continue. Finally, the company's executives mentioned that they are on the verge of joining the small-cap index at B3 (SMLL11) in the future, which we believe could increase the liquidity of the shares.


Macro background. The company expects the capital market to remain dynamic in 2025. Several factors contribute to this positive outlook, including the growing market share of the capital market in relation to banks' corporate credit. Companies are opting to raise funds directly on the capital market rather than relying on traditional bank loans. In addition, the economy continues to heat up, maintaining the need for investment and consequently boosting capital market activity. Refinancing is also an important source of business.


Balanced outlook. Although the bank's activity has a significant cyclical component, it has managed to diversify its sources of income in recent years. BRBI's executives emphasized that the current scenario remains challenging, but some segments are strong, allowing the bank to continue building a healthy pipeline of business.


Taking advantage of opportunities. Although the current debt issuance market scenario may raise concerns, especially in relation to the balance between risk and return, it has also allowed BR Partners to structure deals for its clients and strengthen its capital ratios at attractive costs. We believe this will allow the bank to navigate volatile scenarios and serve increasingly larger clients.


Momentum of results. The company shared that the last few months have maintained a positive trajectory, with healthy revenue and controlled costs. The executives noted that the bank's current level of maturity has allowed it to build a business pipeline with a healthy lead time, resulting in slightly greater revenue predictability. This should allow the company to continue delivering a healthy ROE and distributing a significant portion of its profits (payout above 65%, given the comfortable BIS ratio).


BRBI's differentials. During the discussions, the bank's main differentiators became even clearer: BRBI's ability to customize solutions. The executives stressed that, in the investment banking business, there are opportunities for greater monetization in the short term. However, the bank's culture has always prioritized generating greater value in the long term. Another topic of discussion was how the bank's service stands out from its main competitors. According to them, the ability to structure personalized deals that fully meet clients' needs has allowed the bank to grow over the years.


Valuation and liquidity of shares. The NDR reinforced our positive view of the shares. The earnings momentum should continue, and we still see decent upside potential. In addition, our preliminary calculations suggest that the bank's shares could be included in the next rebalancing of the small-cap index. If this happens, the average trading volume of the bank's shares could benefit. However, it is still too early to make a precise estimate of the potential increase in volume, as the change would only take place in January.


Revenue quality as an important pillar. Revenue quality is improving due to diversification and an increase in recurring products and services. Previously, revenue was concentrated in more volatile activities such as capital markets, but now there is growth in areas such as derivatives and treasury, along with the inclusion of new revenue lines such as wealth management.


Key man risk? The bank's revenue is becoming less dependent on a specific individual (e.g. Ricardo Lacerda). The company has strengthened its connections with clients, and other team members are generating business independently, reducing dependence on key leadership figures.


Restructuring. The restructuring vertical has played a key role in the company's recent growth. Recently established, it complements other consulting areas, such as mergers and acquisitions and board services, and has been crucial in increasing the company's recurring revenue.


Agribusiness. The agribusiness sector is experiencing a different dynamic among its sub-segments. Companies in the grain sector, such as corn and soybeans, are facing challenges, while areas such as food services, corn ethanol and sugar are benefiting from high demand and prices. These movements in the sector are creating opportunities for restructuring, especially for companies that need to adjust their operations and finances due to economic pressures. Rising interest rates and volatility in the agricultural sector in recent years have intensified the need for financial restructuring, boosting the work of consultancies and financial institutions offering services in this area.
