# BR Partners: beginning, challenges and strategy

# BR Partners: beginning, challenges and strategy


BR Partners


Original article:[Access link](https://www.brpartners.com.br/en-US/home.html)


DECEMBER 04, 2024


9 minutes of reading


# We talked with BR Partners' CEO and founder Ricardo Lacerda. As BR Partners, which had its IPO in 2021, recently completed 15 years, this interview focused on the bank's early years, which began its activities as a financial advisory firm.


## How did BR Partners come about?


BR Partners emerged from a dream I had since the beginning of my career, but one that I didn't have, let's say, enough courage to pursue. Throughout my career, I held good positions and climbed my way through large international banks, such as Chase Manhattan, which today is JP Morgan, Goldman Sachs, and Citi. When the 2008 crisis came, I saw its impact on large institutions and how it redesigned the financial market worldwide and in Brazil. At that point, I found the courage to start a business, as I saw it was the moment to make a 180-degree turn in my career and try to build my own business.


## Everything started as an advisory firm, correct?


Everything started as an advisory firm. When we started BR Partners, I talked to many people, and one of those conversations to gather ideas and advice was with Roberto Setúbal from Itaú. The advice he gave me was to do things in a modular way, waiting for one thing to succeed before doing another and so on. With this approach, business lines would be developed in a situation where we would already have profitability. That was advice I really liked, and from the beginning, we've had the mindset of running the marathon step by step. When they asked Mao Tse Tung how he did the 10,000-kilometer march, he said "by taking one step after another."


We started as a financial advisory firm, which was basically the background that I and much of the team had, but already with the design of becoming a complete financial institution. Later, we raised capital of R$100 million and applied for a banking license to operate in the other areas where we are operating today.


## From the beginning, was BR Partners' goal to become a bank?


From the beginning. We went somewhat against the tide of the 2008/2009 crisis, when large regulated players suffered greatly and the market was left with the impression that it would move more toward a situation of deregulation, operating through funds and less heavy structures than a bank. Not us. From the beginning, we had the idea of being a complete financial institution with a full range of products and services. For this, we were clear that we had to do it well, meaning the bank had to have owners and these owners had to be aligned with the institution's risk and the client's interest, as we believed this was the model that would prevail.


## In an extremely competitive sector like banking, did it make sense to open yet another bank?


It did, because we saw that traditional banks were losing competitiveness due to problems with balance sheet size, asset quality, and costs. That's how we decided to create a platform that would correct several of these problems and be competitive. Now, it was a bold move on our part. We entered a segment that is very competitive and where it's difficult to do things.


Building a bank from scratch, as we did, is a major challenge, but we were very clear about where we could make a difference and be competitive. It was around this that we built our entire strategy.


## Did anyone tell you this was crazy?


Several people, including partners. Many said it was crazy, but that's okay, it might have been, but we had well-mapped out what we wanted to do and how we would face all the challenges and make the business work.


## How was BR Partners' first phase?


In the first phase, nothing was easy. Neither in the first phase, nor in the second, nor today. The challenges are constant, especially in a business like ours, which depends a lot on people, reputation, and capital, in that order. In the first phase, we had every kind of challenge you can imagine, like finding space to set up the office. As the market was very heated when we started, this in 2009, early 2010, we borrowed an office from one of our investors, and we even had to relocate him from his space, as we grew rather quickly. In addition, we had challenges such as communicating with clients, attracting people, going through the entire licensing process with the Central Bank, which is quite complex, and investing in systems.


At the beginning, these difficulties are even greater, as it was necessary to do many things at the same time, the structure was small, we were not known to clients, and we had to go out to introduce ourselves, show the platform we were creating, and try to bring in business. Nothing was easy.


## In a market that was already established, how did you manage to win BR Partners' first clients?


We operate in a market that has always been and continues to be extremely competitive. When investors ask me who we compete with, I answer that we compete with everyone, such as local players, international players, small players, large players, pure investment banks, and financial conglomerates.


What we did was to approach companies with which we already had contact to show them that we were an alternative for financial advisory. This is an uphill battle, with a lot of word-of-mouth and presentations. At first, some receive you almost as a favor, since this is a very competitive market with a very large supply of services, but we had confidence that we were offering a good and competitive alternative for our clients with strong alignment with their interests.


## BR Partners started in 2009 as an advisory firm, and in 2013 came the bank. When did you feel the business was gaining traction?


I would separate BR Partners' phases into setup, which was from 2009 to 2013 when the bank came, and we had our first reality check in 2015/2016, during the economic disaster of the Dilma era. The first time we felt the business was gaining traction and growing was in 2014, five years after our start, but then we went through a very strong market shock, which caused activity to fall sharply in 2015/2016. At that moment, we had to take a step back regarding some activities we were starting that would end up being very expensive, and we also reduced the number of people.


From 2017/2018, we began to see a more consistent acceleration of our activity level, when our areas started to gain quite a bit of traction.


## Why did you insist on positioning BR Partners as an independent bank?


Precisely because we saw that large financial conglomerates, after the 2008 Crisis, were exposed to a series of conflicts of interest in their operations, as they can be advisors, creditors, and investors. These many hat changes mean there are many situations that can be conflicting. That's why we wanted to show that BR Partners' interest was aligned with the interests of its clients. For this, the bank is very focused on financial advisory products, with most of our revenue still coming from services, where there is no position of conflict of interest with clients.


This aspect of independence is a very important market positioning for BR Partners as a competitive player. We emphasize this aspect a lot, as it brings results, since people see that, really, we don't have the conflicts that, normally, creditors and investors have. For example, we don't have a proprietary trading desk where we make bets. We are, fundamentally, a service company where our interest is 100% aligned with the interests of our clients.


## In your opinion, why hasn't anyone else repeated this positioning in the Brazilian market?


The Brazilian market has some players that repeat this, but they are more niche than us. For example, the market has some international banks that do niche financial advisory operations and some local boutiques that operate more in the financial advisory area. The point is that we decided to do this on a slightly larger scale. A scale that involved not only financial advisory products but also capital markets, treasury, wealth management, etc.


We extended this independence to several segments on a scale that is not reached by more niche players, but there are indeed other players that position themselves this way, but not with the entire portfolio of products that we have.


## How do you see BR Partners 15 years from now?


I hope that BR Partners will be much larger than we are today. To use a fashionable word, our ecosystem is made up of large companies, businesspeople, entrepreneurs, investors, and a bit of government. There are about 2,000 names within the Brazilian economy for whom we want to offer the widest possible range of products and services.


Today, we actively operate in five segments, but there are others where we can grow. Three, five, ten, fifteen years from now, I would like to extract even more from this ecosystem, serving our clients on the various fronts they need. For example, from this group of 2,000 clients, I believe we already meet approximately 40% of their needs, but we want to get close to 100%. That's how I see BR Partners in the next 15 years.


## Considering our conversation, would you like to add any point to your interview?


At the beginning of an operation, you have trivial challenges such as obtaining the company's registration number (CNPJ), starting to operate, having an accounting person, opening a bank account, renting the office, and hiring people, as well as more complex aspects such as defining the company culture that will establish how we will deal with clients and professionals, how people will work together, and what the firm's overall objective is.


As these challenges grow, they start with the most trivial ones and deepen into aspects that have a longer-term impact, such as defining business strategy and risk management, since the bank has a large portfolio of businesses. However, the greatest of these challenges, in my opinion, is defining the culture.


Today, we have a very established culture of being a service company, of having our interest aligned with our client, and of having very defined the profile of the people we hire, train, and what we expect from these professionals. As culture can define the success and failure of a company very quickly, we always have to be vigilant with it and revisit it. As this is a business of people, reputation, and capital, in that order, we have to ensure these three aspects to remain competitive.
