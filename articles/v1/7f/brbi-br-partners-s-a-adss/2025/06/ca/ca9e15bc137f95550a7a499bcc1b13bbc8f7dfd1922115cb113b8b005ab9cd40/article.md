# BR Partners (BRBI11): 1Q25 results, current situation and outlook

# BR Partners (BRBI11): 1Q25 results, current situation and outlook


BR Partners


Original article:[Access link](https://monitormercantil.com.br/br-partners-brbi11-resultado-do-1t25-momento-e-perspectivas/)


JUNE 13, 2025


8 minutes of reading


We spoke with Vinicius Carmona, partner and Investor Relations Officer of BR Partners, about the bank's Q1 2025 results.


**How does BR Partners evaluate its Q1 2025 results?**


Q1 2025 was already more difficult compared to the quarters of 2024. Although we have once again shown great resilience, thanks to our team that achieved a ROAE of 22%, when we look at revenue, we see a clear slowdown in the level of activity in the business areas. Therefore, the ROAE we had was due to the efficiency and expense control of the bank.


This result already had a more challenging dynamic, mainly due to the effect of the macroeconomic environment. When we consider the current interest rate level, 14.75%, mixed with all this volatility in the international market generated by the trade war, it hinders the progress of our business.
For example, in Investment Banking, although we have the best pipeline in our history, the large mandates we are working on tend to be completed in a future where we have a little more clarity regarding the economic environment we are experiencing. With this cost of debt, executives, families, shareholders, and companies, in many cases, hold back and tend to wait for a better time to eventually make an acquisition, take on debt, or access the capital market.
As a result, the priority becomes taking care of the company's financial health, seeking efficiency and reducing expenses as much as possible to maintain a healthy business margin, with large acquisitions and strategic moves taking a back seat. This, obviously, is reflected in our business, so much so that only one investment banking transaction was announced in Q1 2025.
In the capital market, although we have a very high cost of debt, the bank still has a debt pipeline that remains strong, however contradictory and surprising it may seem. It's worth highlighting that this pipeline isn't for a leveraged buyout, meaning debt for asset acquisition, but rather for debt refinancing and restructuring.
In real estate, where we are very strong, as the high-end segment continues to perform very well, with returns that justify the cost of capital, we have projects that are still being funded. For example, in infrastructure, we are working on a mandate for a major project. These projects that pay for themselves and are still being funded through the capital markets should guarantee a good year for the bank.
When we talk about more complex and transformational projects, such as company mergers or acquisitions of strategic assets, these operations take a back seat. Therefore, we should see a reduction in the pace of M&A, accompanied by a debt market that is still doing well with this high cost of debt, but which at some point this year should begin to slow down, since no individual or company can sustain a debt cost of 15% plus the spread for a long period.


In this environment of high volatility, even though treasury is an area that is fluctuating more and more, we have managed to navigate well between periods of drought and periods with higher production. For example, we made many hedges for currency protection in 1Q25, given the high volatility of the dollar. Even so, life for the treasury is not easy, on the contrary, it is quite challenging, since capital market issuances have become more inconsistent. The Treasury will be able to get through the year well, but with less linearity.


Finally, Wealth Management continues to grow and is doing very well. We already have R$ 5.5 billion of wealth under advisor and our internal goal, which is not a market guidance, is to reach close to R$ 10 billion by the end of this year. We are well positioned to grow, but this is a line that represents only 3% of revenues, that is, we need scale so that we can make this business more representative.


**Does BR Partners' performance usually show seasonality?**


Given all the uncertainty, we should expect more volatility in the results of the coming quarters. Since the IPO, we have had a reasonably linear ROAE, but since we are now at such a high level of uncertainty, we may have one quarter with significantly lower profitability and another exceptional one, should the scenario improve and give confidence to companies.


For this year, we should have less linear results, but not due to seasonality. One could even say that in December and January, when decision-makers generally take vacations, major decisions are not made, but I wouldn't say there is a clear seasonality in our business, but rather the effect of the stability or instability of the economy at the moment.


Currently, we have a bad situation, where the cost of debt is extremely high, the country has major fiscal challenges that need to be addressed, next year there are elections, and the international scenario is chaotic. With so much uncertainty, we should start to feel, mainly through M&A, a reduction in announced transactions. Now, this is also uncertain, as the game can change from one month to the next. For example, we had more positive news that Trump is looking to ease tariffs, which is encouraging the market, which may eventually reprice a less intense global slowdown. All of this can create some triggers so that we can start converting some transactions and have a scenario that, by the end of the year, could improve significantly.


The point is that we are very prepared to face a very challenging year given our structure and size, and considering that 2/3 of our expenses relate to salaries and bonuses. We are unlikely to repeat the 2024 result in terms of profit, but we can have a ROAE above the cost of capital in 2025. In a bad environment like this, generating alpha relative to the Selic rate means that we are generating value for the shareholder.


**How does BR Partners evaluate its payout and dividend yield?**


BR Partners is a dividend-paying company and should continue with a very high payout. We always decide on the payout close to the dividend payment event, as it depends a lot on the moment we are seeing and the real need to pay a little more or a little less, but we should continue paying a payout above 70% in 2025. This is an alignment that we have very close to the minority investor, since our partnership is mostly remunerated via dividends.


It is worth noting that this is why we are able to have great expense control, because from the moment that the bulk of the partner's remuneration is based on the bank's profit, this is a wonderful alignment, as they will receive if they actually produce. For example, if revenue falls due to a bad environment, the result will be impacted and the partner will receive less "dividends". Thus, since everyone wants to be well compensated with the best possible dividends, everyone will fight for a year with good revenues, and at the end of the day, the dividends that the partners receive are the same as those received by the minority shareholders.
In our business, there is no risk of having a terrible year with highly compensated management, which would burden the minority shareholder. Similarly, if there is a year with an extremely good market, we do not have an overpayment of executives, as everyone benefits from the dividends. With this, there is no fight for "I generated R$ 40 million, so I have to earn R$ 5 million in bonuses." That doesn't exist here.
In addition to the discipline of being aligned with dividends, we have an internal policy of paying, at most, what we call a 30% remuneration index, that is, this is the maximum limit on revenue that can be spent on salaries and bonuses. For example, if BR Partners has a revenue of R$ 100 million, it spends R$ 30 million on salaries and bonuses for its employees. This is also a great comfort for minority shareholders, as we limit salaries and bonuses to revenue generation.
Regarding dividend yield, I don't like to talk about it much, as it is a function of the share price. For example, you may be paying a lot in dividends, but if the share price has appreciated significantly, this is not very evident. Similarly, if the share price falls sharply, you will pay a brutal dividend yield. That's why I prefer to look at the payout ratio rather than the dividend yield metric.


Recently, as our stock has appreciated significantly, our dividend yield has fallen from 14% last year to 9% now. This is why it is very difficult to predict the dividend yield. For example, if we have a dividend yield of 7% with the stock appreciating, that's great for the minority shareholder, as it's a combination of dividends plus stock appreciation, but 7% isn't necessarily a nice dividend number.


The important thing is that we are in the best moment as a company. We have an extremely aligned partnership and an exceptional pipeline of mandates in Investment Banking, waiting for the best time to exit. Although challenges are present, we are prepared for a growth avenue as soon as the economy improves somewhat.
