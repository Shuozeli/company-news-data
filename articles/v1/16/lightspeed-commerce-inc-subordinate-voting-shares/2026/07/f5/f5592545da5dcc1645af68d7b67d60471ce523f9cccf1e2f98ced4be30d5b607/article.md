## What is the purpose of a golf course website?


While “marketing” may seem like the obvious answer, a golf course website serves another equally important purpose: it’s a business tool designed to generate revenue. Its primary goal should be to make it as easy as possible for golfers to go from discovering your course to booking a tee time.


Once that foundation is in place, your website can support a range of secondary goals, such as booking lessons, selling memberships and packages, promoting tournaments and events, showcasing amenities, growing your email list and more.


**Simply put:** if the purpose of a golf course website is to drive tee time bookings, then the best golf course website design removes friction at every step of the booking journey.

## 1. Make tee time booking the primary call to action


As we’ve established, the primary goal of your golf course website should be generating tee time bookings. That means making the booking process visible, intuitive and accessible from the moment a golfer lands on your site.


To achieve this, follow a few key best practices:


- Place a “Book a Tee Time” button in your website header
- Add a booking widget that remains accessible across the site
- Feature a prominent booking CTA in your homepage hero section
- Include booking buttons on other high-traffic pages to reduce unnecessary clicks


Just as importantly, make sure your booking buttons stand out. Avoid blending them into your site’s design by using contrasting colors that naturally draw the eye. For example, if your navigation uses black text on a white background, consider an inverted button style to make your primary call to action immediately recognizable.


The easier it is for golfers to find your booking button, the more likely they are to book.


## 2. Use high-quality photos and videos


Your website is a visual representation of your facility, so the images you choose matter. Generic stock photos—or worse, AI-generated images—can quickly undermine trust and make it harder for golfers to picture the experience your course offers.


Instead, use high-quality, up-to-date visuals that showcase what makes your facility unique. Consider featuring:


- Signature holes
- Current course conditions
- Clubhouse facilities
- Dining spaces and signature dishes
- Practice areas


Photos aren’t your only option. Many leading golf courses also use short videos and drone footage to bring their property to life and give prospective golfers a better sense of the on-course experience.


Strong visuals don’t just make your website more attractive—they help golfers imagine their visit and build confidence before they ever book a tee time.


## 3. Keep navigation simple and intuitive


Nothing frustrates website visitors more than struggling to find the information they’re looking for. That’s why golf course websites should prioritize simple, intuitive navigation that helps golfers reach key pages with as few clicks as possible.


Your website’s navigation should make it easy to find:


- Tee times


- Rates


- Memberships


- Events


- Course information


- Contact details


As a general rule, if a first-time visitor can’t quickly find what they need, your navigation is likely too complicated. A clean, well-organized menu creates a better user experience and makes golfers more likely to book.


## 4. Provide detailed course information


While high-quality images help showcase your course, golfers also need practical information before they’re ready to book. The more questions your website answers upfront, the easier it is for visitors to book with confidence.


Make sure your website includes information such as:


- Course overview and layout
- Yardage, hole descriptions and scorecards
- Green fees and pricing
- Practice facilities
- Course conditions (when possible)
- Pace of play expectations
- Dress code and course policies
- Rental information (clubs, carts and push carts)
- Dining and clubhouse amenities
- Directions, parking and contact information


Some courses also provide downloadable scorecards or course guides to help golfers prepare for their round.


Providing easy-to-find information doesn’t just improve the golfer experience—it can also help your website rank better in search results by giving search engines and AI tools more useful content to understand and surface to searchers.

## 5. Clearly display rates, promos and offers


Green fees are a major factor when deciding where to book a round of golf. If your website doesn’t list your prices clearly, golfers are likely to find a competitor that can provide better information on how the associated costs breakdown.


Clearly listing up-to-date rates for everything from a 9-hole game to an annual membership will ultimately establish transparency and enable golfers to make their booking decisions faster. Golf courses should also use their websites to promote special offers, such as:


- Twilight specials


- Gamepacks


- Group rates


- Seasonal deals


- Membership incentives


- Stay-and-play packages


- Lesson packages


## 6. Build a strong brand on your golf course website


Providing the right information is only part of creating a great golf course website. Equally important is building a cohesive brand identity that makes your facility stand out.


A strong brand goes beyond your logo. It should be reflected consistently across your website through elements such as:


- Color palette


- Typography


- Photography and visual style


- Tone of voice and messaging


- Imagery that highlights your course’s unique character


For example, a seaside golf course might lean into coastal colors and imagery, while a parkland course could emphasize lush landscapes and natural surroundings.


Most importantly, your branding should be consistent both online and on property. When golfers arrive at your course, the experience should feel like a natural extension of what they saw on your website, helping build familiarity, trust and recognition.


## 7. Prioritize mobile performance


With mobile searches and bookings on the rise, optimizing your website for smartphones is no longer optional—it’s essential. In fact, according to an analysis of Lightspeed tee time booking traffic,


**84% of online tee time reservation sessions come from mobile devices** , either through a mobile app or mobile web browser.


A mobile-friendly golf course website should offer:


- Fast page load speeds


- Simple, intuitive navigation


- Large, easy-to-tap buttons


- A seamless mobile booking experience


- Readable text and responsive layouts


Think about the golfer searching for a nearby tee time while out with friends. They should be able to go from searching for your course to completing a booking in just a few taps.


Regularly test your website on mobile devices to ensure key workflows—from finding rates to booking a tee time—remain fast and frustration-free. A smooth mobile experience doesn’t just improve usability; it can directly translate into more bookings.


## 8. Optimize for search engines and AI search


Even the best-designed golf course website won’t generate bookings if golfers can’t find it. That’s why search engine optimization (SEO) and online discoverability should be part of every website strategy.


Strong SEO starts with the fundamentals, including:


- Fast page load speeds


- Clear, descriptive page titles


- Accurate location and contact information


- A logical site structure


- Helpful, informative content such as FAQs, course details and local information


Creating useful content is especially important as AI-powered search continues to grow. Rather than searching with a few keywords, golfers are increasingly asking conversational questions like,


*“What’s the best 18-hole golf course near me?”* or


*“Where can I book a tee time in Michigan today?”*


Websites that provide clear, comprehensive answers to these types of questions are more likely to appear in both traditional search results and AI-generated responses. By focusing on helpful content—not just keywords—you can improve your visibility and make it easier for golfers to discover your course.


## Common golf course website mistakes to avoid


If your golf course website isn’t delivering the results you’d hoped for, it could be down to a number of issues. In fact, most underperforming websites are impacted by a combination of friction points that can be resolved with some oversight and regular updates.


#### **Common mistakes include:**


- **Outdated design** : this can make your business look less active and suggest that information listed is no longer relevant.


- **Slow page speeds** : lagging load times will deter potential customers and increase bounce rates.


- **Opaque booking options** : if a golfer is struggling to book a tee time on your website, chances are they’ll look elsewhere.


- **Low-quality imagery** : generic or low-resolution photos can make your business seem unprofessional.


- **Confusing navigation** : unintuitive website menus can increase friction and drive potential customers away.


- **Outdated information** : if vital data like opening times, rates, and promotions aren’t up-to-date, customers will lose trust in your business.


- **Broken links** : similarly, a broken link will cause frustration to users and break brand trust. Broken links also impact SEO performance.


- **Weak SEO foundations** : neglecting SEO optimization will make your website hard to find and prioritize competitors on search engines.


- **Poor mobile experiences** : failing to address the mobile user experience can cause navigation issues, booking hurdles, and overall poor brand reputation.

For many golf courses, the answer is yes.


Compared to a fully custom website, template-based designs are typically faster to launch, more affordable and easier to maintain. Modern website templates also come with many of the features golfers expect, including responsive mobile design, streamlined navigation and built-in best practices for usability.


The key is choosing a template that’s designed specifically for golf courses. This gives you a strong foundation while still allowing you to customize your branding, colors, imagery and messaging to create a website that reflects your facility’s unique identity.


Ultimately, the best templates combine professional design with the flexibility to grow alongside your business.

## Golf Course Website Design FAQs


#### **What should a golf course website include?**


A strong golf course website will always include the following information and capabilities: tee time booking, rates and promotions, contact information, membership details, and course information (i.e. holes, layout, yardage, and course ratings). Additionally, high-quality imagery and mobile-optimized design are integral to the user experience.


#### **Why is website design important for golf courses?**


Website design can be the difference between a tee time booking and a golfer who clicks away to a competitor. Good golf course website design prioritizes a seamless user experience—with a focus on facilitating tee time bookings—while also establishing a strong brand and giving golfers a glimpse of the experience to come.


#### **How can a golf course website increase tee time bookings?**


A well designed golf course website can increase tee time bookings by drawing golfers in with high-quality imagery and orienting them to make a booking. The latter is achieved using prominent and always-visible booking buttons, an easy-to-navigate menu, and transparent pricing.


#### **Are golf course website design templates a good option?**


Yes, golf courses can benefit from using a website design template rather than building a custom website from scratch. Generally, website templates are faster to design and more affordable, while still allowing for a degree of customization (i.e. colors, typeface, branding, imagery). They also facilitate mobile compatibility.


#### **How important is mobile optimization for golf course websites?**


With more and more golfers relying on their mobile devices to find golf courses and book tee times, mobile optimization for golf course websites is a must. Moreover, because many mobile users have strong booking intent, golf courses have to offer them a seamless user experience, in which reservations can be made with minimal clicks.


#### **How can golf courses improve their visibility in Google and AI search results?**


Ranking well on Google is key to driving website traffic and bookings. Golf courses can improve their rankings by optimizing website content for SEO. This means providing up-to-date information, a logical website structure, and fast loading times. With AI search tools in particular, providing content that answers questions (such as an FAQ section) can help drive search engine performance.


#### **How often should a golf course website be updated?**


Golf course websites should be updated frequently, with blogs updated at least once a month, pricing and event information updated as needed, and performance tests conducted a couple times a year. Imagery should be updated one or twice a year, while design and layout changes can be done every couple of years. Updates should be made based on performance and user behavior metrics.


#### **Should golf courses hire a professional website design partner?**


Golf courses can benefit from hiring a professional website design partner particularly if they don’t have in-house marketing or web development teams. Having a dedicated website design partner can ensure that your golf course’s online performance is optimized for user experience, SEO, and conversions. In the long run, this type of support can save businesses time and resources, while also freeing up golf course staff to focus on the facility’s operation.
