Oracle’s upcoming sunset of NetSuite POS and SuiteCommerce InStore (SCIS) has forced a sudden pivot for many retailers. While an unexpected software migration like this is always a major disruption, it’s also a massive strategic opportunity. Forward-thinking retailers won’t simply be scrambling for a quick, 1:1 POS replacement. They’ll use this moment to re-evaluate their entire commerce stack, and ensure they’re making the right decisions for the next decade of retail.


With that said, we’ve identified three core areas that retailers should prioritize as they plan their post-sunset roadmaps. Keep reading as we outline these priorities and uncover critical considerations that will be important as you navigate this transition yourself.


## **Priority 1: Strengthening ERP-connected operations**


For any enterprise retailer, the NetSuite ERP acts as the ultimate source of truth for financials, procurement and high-level inventory tracking. The biggest risk during a platform swap is degrading that source of truth by introducing data silos or fragile integration layers.


Growth-minded brands should filter their software search through a strict operational lens, focusing on three specific criteria:


- **Real-time, bidirectional sync:** When an item sells in store, a purchase order is adjusted or a return is processed, that data needs to sync with NetSuite immediately. Retailers should prioritize platforms that offer live, two-way syncing so that teams always have an accurate, real-time view of their inventory and profitability.


- **Eliminating custom code middleware:** Custom-built API integrations are expensive to build and highly fragile. To mitigate implementation risk, companies should prioritize platforms with native or deeply established NetSuite connectors—specifically those carrying official Built for NetSuite (BFN) certification. This ensures the integration meets Oracle’s enterprise standards for security without requiring internal teams to maintain custom code.


- **Protecting business continuity:** A successful transition means front-of-house staff experience zero friction or learning curves on day one of the new system, while back-of-house teams see a clean continuation of automatic transaction matching, tax management and cash reconciliation.

## **Priority 2: Unifying retail, omnichannel and wholesale ecosystems**


Consumers rarely interact with their favorite brands through a single channel. And many NetSuite users already run highly complex operations that span brick-and-mortar, ecommerce, B2B and more. Legacy retail systems would force these environments into separate operational silos, creating a disjointed flow of data back to the central ERP. But this doesn’t work for modern retailers and especially not for those that plan to scale.


To build a truly unified commerce model that works now


*and* in the future, brands should leverage this migration period as an opportunity to clean up their infrastructure with seamlessly connected tech.


### **Gaining a complete, transparent view of inventory**


Managing inventory across a modern retail business of any size requires complete, real-time visibility across every physical storefront, warehouse and digital sales channel. Without this, execution falls short and leads to detrimental out-of-stock or oversold scenarios.


During this migration period, retailers should prioritize systems that seamlessly bridge their physical store stock with their digital storefronts. These modern systems ensure that exact, live inventory counts are accessible across all locations simultaneously. This is crucial to executing omnichannel fulfillment options like buy online, pick up in store (BOPIS), curbside pickup or ship-from-store, for example.


### **Connecting customer journeys**


Customers expect their brand relationship to be recognized everywhere. If a customer accumulates loyalty points online or updates their profile details via their mobile device, that data should be instantly accessible to an associate on the physical showroom floor. True cross-channel visibility empowers associates to sell more and drives retention, making it an essential consideration during this migration period.


### **Bridging the retail and wholesale divide**


For scaling brands, the purchasing side of retail is historically fragmented. Buyers spend hours flipping through catalogs, manually drafting purchase orders and hand-keying new stock descriptions once shipments arrive. As you migrate from NSPOS or SCSIS to another system, you don’t have to maintain this same disconnected process.


A solution like


**Lightspeed Retail for NetSuite** has a built-in wholesale network, allowing buyers to view live supplier catalogs and generate POs directly from sales reports. Because the system is built to organize complex inventory—including high-volume SKUs, variants and serialized items—retailers gain clear visibility into exact stock counts across every stockroom, warehouse and digital storefront.


When a shipment arrives, staff can simply scan it in to update counts across all locations, and automatically update values in NetSuite. What’s more, access to live sales data and trend insights make it easier to forecast seasonal demand and identify bestsellers early. By pairing these insights with automated low-stock alerts and preset reorder points, retailers can keep shelves balanced, improve sell-through and reduce deadstock without additional manual work.

## **Priority 3: Scalability beyond immediate needs**


With a sunset deadline approaching, it’s easy to fall into the trap of picking a POS platform just to cross it off your to-do list. However, switching your front-of-house infrastructure is a


*major* investment of time, resources and capital. Having to repeat the entire process three years from now because your business outgrew a short-sighted solution is an operational failure.


Retailers focused on sustainable, long-term expansion are evaluating potential software partners based on their capacity for future scale. They’ll be considering:


- **Geographic and location flexibility:** Opening a new permanent location, launching a seasonal pop-up or expanding into a new international market shouldn’t require your IT department to start from scratch. Growth-minded retailers are looking for platforms that enable them to add new sales channels or physical locations seamlessly. The connection to your NetSuite ERP should be plug-and-play, automatically adapting to your new locations without requiring expensive custom workarounds.


- **Unlocking richer, multi-store insights:** While the ERP remains the system of record for macro financials, store managers and regional directors need clear and accessible analytics to make day-to-day decisions. Growth-minded retailers should choose platforms that deliver robust yet intuitive reports on product performance and staff scheduling to help their teams at the store level.


## **The strategic path forward**


The retailers that will benefit most from the end of NetSuite POS and SCIS are those who refuse to treat the sunset like any other IT chore. By looking beyond a simple software swap, these brands will set themselves up for years of supported growth. They’re choosing platforms that streamline processes and eliminate tedious admin tasks, so their staff can spend less time fixing data errors and more time doing what they do best.
