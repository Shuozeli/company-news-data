# **Solo Traveller Honest Review of zingbus Delhi to Manali Bus**


Delhi to Manali by bus covers roughly 566 km and takes between 10 and 14 hours overnight, depending on traffic near Chandigarh and the climb past Mandi. As a Senior Content Writer at zingbus, I spend a lot of time researching routes and writing about travel experiences across India. But this particular journey had always been different for me because I had only ever driven it in my own car. I picked zingbus Plus for this trip because it sits in the comfort range most travellers actually choose, with reclining seats, blankets, and charging ports, without the full luxury positioning of a premium coach.


Driving this route myself always meant controlling every variable, when to stop, when to push through. This time, I wanted to experience the journey the way most travellers do. Handing that control over to someone else felt like the best way to understand what this route is really like from a passenger’s perspective rather than from behind the wheel.


## **How I Booked My Seat and Why I Picked It**


I booked five days ahead through the app and chose a window recliner seat toward the front third of the bus, past the wheel arch but ahead of the rear section where road vibration tends to be strongest on hill climbs. zingbus Plus runs a comfortable semi sleeper layout rather than the flat berths you get on a full AC sleeper coach, which suited a first overnight bus trip better than I expected.


The 6 PM departure from Kashmiri Gate worked well with my schedule, giving me a full night on the road before waking up somewhere in the hills. The fare came in reasonably below what a full sleeper coach would have cost, which is exactly the trade off zingbus Plus is built around.


## **What I Saw When I Reached the Kashmiri Gate Boarding Point**


Kashmiri Gate at 5:40 PM was better organised than I expected for someone used to just parking his own car and walking off. A proper lit waiting area, several zingbus departures being called out one after another, and a marshal working from a tablet rather than a printed sheet, checking names against the passenger list without any real delay.


I noticed a woman travelling alone a few places ahead of me in the queue, and she had booked one of the women only seats through the app, seated deliberately next to another solo female passenger rather than left to chance. It is a small detail, but a meaningful one if you are the person actually relying on it for a night journey into unfamiliar territory.


## **How Boarding Actually Went for Me as a First Time Passenger**


Boarding started a little before 6 PM, and for someone used to simply getting into his own car, the process took a moment to adjust to. The marshal checked my ticket, matched the OTP sent to my phone, and pointed me to my seat without confusion, the same process I watched him repeat for an elderly couple a few names ahead of me who needed an extra minute to find their booking on their phone.


If you are travelling by overnight bus for the first time, this is roughly what to expect: a passenger list, an ID check, an OTP confirmation, and a seat assignment that matches exactly what you booked. None of it felt complicated once I actually went through it, though I understand why it feels unfamiliar before you have done it even once.


## **What I Noticed on the Road Out of Delhi**


We pulled away from Kashmiri Gate at around 6 PM, right on schedule, making for a smooth start to the journey. About twenty minutes in, the marshal walked through the bus, confirming the charging ports were working and checking that everyone had settled into the correct seat, a habit I noticed him repeat consistently rather than as a one-time formality.


Sitting in a recliner rather than a flat berth meant I was more aware of the road than I would have been asleep in a sleeper coach, which actually suited the point of this trip. I could watch how the driver handled the stretch past Karnal, how the marshal managed the rest stop timing, all the details I would have missed entirely behind my own wheel.


## **Why I Pre-Ordered My Meal Before the Journey**


While booking my Delhi to Manali bus ticket on the zingbus app, I noticed the option to pre-order a meal for the journey. Since it was an overnight trip, I preferred knowing that dinner was already taken care of instead of relying on a highway stop or searching for food later in the evening.


I selected the **Premium Veg Thali** while completing my booking, and it was delivered to my seat during the journey. It was convenient not having to get off the bus or rush through a short halt just to grab a meal.


The **Food On Bus** service on zingbus Plus lets you pre-book your meal along with your ticket, making the experience more seamless for long-distance travel. It is different from the complimentary multi-course meal that comes with zingbus Maxx.


For a route like Delhi to Manali, where you’re travelling overnight, pre-ordering my meal turned out to be one of those small conveniences that made the journey more comfortable from the very beginning.


## **What Happened at the Rest Stop Near Karnal**


The rest stop near Karnal came a little after 9 PM. The marshal gave a clear fifteen-minute window before departure and repeated it once more as passengers stepped off, then did a headcount before we pulled out again rather than assuming everyone had made it back in time. The stop itself was clean, well-lit, and had maintained washrooms along with a few food counters, making it a comfortable place to stretch after the first few hours on the road.


I watched him answer a specific question from another passenger about the next stop, giving an actual estimate rather than a vague answer. Small interactions like that told me more about how the crew operates than anything I could have researched beforehand from behind a steering wheel.


## **What I’d Tell a Solo Female Traveller Based on What I Actually Saw**


I am not the person best placed to speak to how this route feels specifically for a woman travelling alone, and I do not want to pretend otherwise. What I can report is what I actually saw: women only seating booked and honoured exactly as shown in the app, a marshal present and visible throughout the night, and a boarding point that was lit and staffed rather than a dark roadside guess.


If you are a solo female traveller weighing this route, those are the concrete details worth knowing, not a vague reassurance from someone who has not lived that specific concern himself.


## **What I Experienced Somewhere Past Mandi**


Network coverage dropped well before Mandi, somewhere close to 2 AM by my phone’s last update. The road narrowed, the turns sharpened, and the temperature outside dropped noticeably enough that I was glad for the blanket that comes standard with zingbus Plus. The marshal came through once more during this stretch, checking window latches and blankets without any prompting.


Driving this same stretch myself always meant white knuckling the turns in the dark. Being a passenger through it, watching someone else manage the road while I just watched the hills go by, was honestly the most convincing argument for taking the bus that the whole trip gave me.


## **How My Arrival in Manali Actually Felt**


The bus reached Manali a little after 10 AM, close enough to the scheduled window that my plans for the day did not need adjusting. Stepping off into properly cold mountain air after a warm, slightly stuffy overnight ride felt different from every previous arrival I have had by car, mostly because I had nothing left to do except walk away.


The marshal helped unload luggage by matching tags to passengers, and I walked off having spent zero energy on the actual driving, navigation, or fuel stops that usually eat into my first day whenever I arrive by car.


## **Where I Stayed and What I Did Once I Got to Manali**


Old Manali, across the river from the main town, is where I based myself for three days. Hadimba Temple, sitting inside genuinely old cedar forest, is worth an early visit before the crowds arrive, and Solang Valley is best tackled before mid morning once the season picks up. Three days felt like enough to actually see the place rather than just pass through it, which is more than I can say for most of my previous drive through visits.

## **What I’d Pack Differently Next Time**


A few things I learned as a first time bus passenger on this route, despite having driven it more times than I can count.


- **Power bank:** Signal drops well before Mandi, and I underestimated how much battery a phone burns searching for a connection.
- **Snacks for the second half:** Rest stops thin out once the climb begins, something I never had to think about while driving my own car with a boot full of supplies.
- **Ticket and ID in an outer pocket:** Boarding moves faster when you are not the person digging through a bag at the front of the queue.
- **A proper second layer:** The temperature drop past Mandi surprised me more as a passenger watching it through a window than it ever did gripping a steering wheel.

This trip was always meant to be a mid tier comparison, and zingbus Plus did exactly what it promised, comfortable, reliable, and considerably cheaper than driving once you account for fuel and my own exhaustion the next day. But watching the premium coaches roll past at the boarding point and hearing other passengers talk about the flat sleeper berths and onboard meals on zingbus Maxx left me curious enough that I already know which tier I am booking for my next trip on this route.
