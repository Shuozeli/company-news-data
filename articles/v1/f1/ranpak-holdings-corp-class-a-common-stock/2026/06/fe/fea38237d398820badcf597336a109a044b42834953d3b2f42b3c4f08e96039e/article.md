# Cold Chain Packaging for the Fresh & Frozen Pet Food Boom

Delivering fresh and frozen pet food to someone’s door sounds straightforward. Doing it profitably, at scale, without compromising temperature integrity or generating a box full of unrecyclable waste is a different problem entirely. The overall rise in cold chain delivery globally has, in part, been caused by surging demand for fresh and frozen pet meal kit delivery over the last five years. It’s a segment that was expected to decline, due to economic pressure on household budgets. Indeed, the rapid growth in fresh and frozen pet food seen between 2020 and 2025 has slowed recently, the category accounts for about 7% of total cat and dog food sales in the US and continues to expand at a[projected 15% – 20% annual rate](https://www.petfoodprocessing.net/articles/20017-fresh-pet-food-growth-is-maturing-not-declining) . Inevitably, while many sales still occur in brick-and-mortar stores, there is a corresponding rise in e-commerce of fresh pet food – and this adds complexity in maintaining refrigeration and consistent logistics infrastructures that meet customer expectations.

Fresh and frozen pet food delivery always seemed like a niche and luxury category but with a reported increase in fresh pet food delivery of[30-40% since 2022](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/fresh-pet-food-market-in-the-us-to-grow-at-a-cagr-of-29-34-from-2022-to-2027expansion-of-fresh-pet-food-vendors-is-the-primary-market-trend---technavio-301867438.html) , far outpacing the broader pet food markets, and with new entrants to the market every month, this is a category that is steadily growing. However, the logistics burden of this category is outsized compared to normal pet food deliveries because it requires strict cold chain management at every step, driving up packaging complexity and cost. Since customers are ultimately price-sensitive, cold chain packaging cannot be a mere supporting function; it must become a critical enabler woven directly into the entire business operation.


## **The Fulfillment Challenge: Scaling Cold Chain for E-Commerce**


Portrait of Whippet dog enjoying a dog-friendly ice cream from dog owner on a sunny day, outdoors at a park in, licking out of the tub in the sunshine, Cornwall, UK


Online pet food delivery brands must navigate several interconnected challenges:


**Vulnerable Infrastructure:** The Direct to Customer delivery infrastructure cannot be fragmented and slow. Setting up last mile delivery systems leaves brands vulnerable to disruptions ranging from adverse weather and traffic delays to mechanical failures.


**The Cost of Thermal Performance:** The cold chain packaging used needs to maintain strict temperatures within the delivery window. Historically, this has triggered overpackaging, inflating costs to both the shipper and the customer.


**Regulatory Pressure:** The evolution of regulations on the sustainability and recyclability of packaging in Asia-Pacific, North America, and Europe, increases complexity and boosts costs.


The increased shipment frequency of these products means that operations must scale effortlessly, often while handling smaller orders, using packaging that balances strict cost-efficiency with high performance output. This is no easy feat: In this sector, cold chain packaging is where cost, critical performance, and customer experience intersect.


## **Why the Time is Right to Reevaluate Cold Chain Packaging**


As discussed in Ranpak’s look into[cold chain packaging within the medical and pharmaceutical industries](https://www.ranpak.com/blog/paper-eps-medical-pharmaceutical-cold-chain/) , cold chain packaging has been dominated by EPS (Expanded Polystyrene or Styrofoam) containers. While EPS containers offer excellent thermal resistance justified for certain medical shipping applications, they represent unsustainable “overkill” for most fresh food deliveries, and the environmental impact of their plastic construction and low recyclability is an issue.


EPS containers are also facing intense scrutiny from regulators and customers alike due to their poor recyclability and heavy landfill footprint.


These pressures mean that packagers must be smarter about cold chain packaging, especially in scenarios just like this: delivering cold or frozen pet meals quickly and safely, at a cost that suits both brands customers, with recyclability at its core.


## **Where New Innovations with Paper Fit**


Paper-based cold chain solutions are maturing quickly to become a direct alternative to EPS containers in many scenarios, such as short-duration shipments. These solutions signal a shift away from over-engineered EPS containers towards packages tailored for their specific delivery lanes. Data-driven packaging designs, engineered and tested to match the transit times and temperature profiles, are reducing material use, lowering costs, and elevating the customer experience through effortless disposal and recycling.


Hybrid solutions that combine paper and bio-based Phase Change Materials (PCMs) are coming into more common use, reducing weight, cost and increasing recyclability. The dramatically reduced use of plastic for both padding and the cartons themselves works positively towards the burgeoning regulations already in place or about to be implemented.


Using paper for cold chain packaging doesn’t need to be a compromise between sustainability and performance. Instead, the right packaging configurations are able to balance thermal performance, operational efficiency, and sustainability in a way that aligns with business priorities including cost and regulatory compliance.
