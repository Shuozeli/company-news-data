## The High Cost of Empty Space


Traditional packaging processes that rely on manual box erection will struggle with excess voids, lower throughput, and higher shipping costs. Not only is the work of creating boxes repetitive and ergonomically challenging, but the need to match the sizes of increasingly diverse order combinations can often lead to ballooning inventories of stock box footprints, especially in environments where mixed shipments are common.


The result? Shipping “air” in the form of excess void. Items need to remain protected despite these voids, which then requires more dunnage material. These poorly optimized boxes take up valuable transport space, and each oversized box incurs higher dimensional (DIM) weight-based shipping fees.


An intelligent rightsizing strategy addresses these inefficiencies head-on. By optimizing the box size to fit the actual contents of an order, businesses can achieve measurable ROI across the board:


- **Material Savings:** Reduce corrugated use and void fill by up to 50%.
- **Cost & Freight Efficiency:** Lower shipping costs by 15–25% and improve truck load efficiency.
- **Labor Productivity:** Remove manual bottlenecks and increase throughput.
- **Sustainability:** Cut carbon emissions and align with emerging EPR and PPWR regulations by reducing shipped void, directly reducing the trucks needed to ship the same number of orders.


## Assessing Different Methods of Rightsizing


The automation methods used to achieve rightsizing vary. Different strategies are in play between 3-D “Box-First” (pre-pick) and “Box-Last” (post-pick) methodologies, while both may struggle with real-world variables like bad SKU data or irregular product shapes. A good example can be found in the individual cube data of a bowl and a cup versus the reality that they can be nested in a box to consolidate their cubic volumes.


Ranpak’s approach— **Intelligent Rightsizing** —bridges this gap. Instead of custom-fabricating every box from scratch based on cubing data, our systems use an active height-reduction method that can be paired with SKU analysis, resulting in a consolidated range of base box footprints, such as a set of Small, Medium, and Large. It’s common for businesses to reduce their footprints from double digit numbers of SKUs down to four or less.


Solutions like the **[Cut’it!™ EVO](https://www.ranpak.com/automated-solutions/evo-cutit/)** machine identify the highest point of filling in a packed box, automatically crease the sides to create flaps, and then fold the box down to the exact height of the products before sealing with a glued lid. This ensures a snug fit, minimizes the need for void fill, and creates a highly stable, pallet-friendly package.


## Innovations & Pressures Driving the Industry


For logistics and operations leaders, the imperative to modernize packaging processes now extends beyond simple cost savings or process efficiency. Regulatory mandates—specifically Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) and the EU’s Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR)—require concrete action to reduce packaging waste, improve recyclability, and demonstrate proactive compliance. Some core deadlines include January 1st, 2030, when all packaging materials are required to be designed for recyclability, and a 50% limit in allowable shipping void also goes into effect.
