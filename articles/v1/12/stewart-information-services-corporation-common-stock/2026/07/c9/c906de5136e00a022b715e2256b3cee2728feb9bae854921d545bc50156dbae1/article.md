# Stewart | Mid-Summer Home Maintenance Checklist

With the summer sun blazing, it's the ideal time to address your mid-summer home maintenance checklist. Regular upkeep ensures your home remains a safe and comfortable space, preventing small issues from escalating into costly repairs.


Retaining and increasing the value of your home also requires recurring updates.


This guide will help you efficiently manage tasks both outdoors and indoors, keeping your property in top condition during the height of summer. From inspecting your roof and gutters to refreshing indoor spaces, we've got all areas covered.


Whether you’re an experienced homeowner or a first-time buyer this summer home maintenance checklist is beneficial to everyone. Looking for ways to cut back on cleaning and upkeep year-round? ## Outdoor Home Summer Maintenance


1. Gutters: Inspect and remove debris to ensure proper drainage and prevent water damage to your roof and foundation.
2. Sprinkler System: Inspect and adjust sprinkler heads to ensure efficient watering and avoid waste.
3. Exterior Paint: Address peeling or chipped paint to protect siding from moisture and sun damage.
4. Pool: Clean the pool,[check chemical levels](https://www.troublefreepool.com/blog/2018/12/12/abcs-of-pool-water-chemistry/) and ensure the pump and filtration systems are working properly.
5. Foundation: Look for cracks and seal them to prevent water seepage and pest entry.
6. Furniture: Inspect outdoor furniture for signs of wear or damage and store it before inclement weather to streamline cleanup and protect against damage.
7. Lighting: Replace bulbs and clean fixtures to ensure safety and enhance visibility at night.
8. Trees and Shrubs: Prune overgrown branches to prevent damage during storms and improve curb appeal.
9. Windows and Doors: Inspect seals and weather stripping for damage, replacing them as needed to prevent leaks and enhance energy efficiency.
10. Faucets and Hoses: Inspect for leaks and make repairs to avoid water wastage and potential water damage.


## Indoor Home Maintenance Tips


1. Air Conditioner: Clean or replace filters to improve efficiency and airflow, ensuring your home stays cool during the summer months.
2. Dryer Machine: Remove lint buildup from the filter and vent tube to prevent fire hazards and enhance dryer efficiency.
3. Washing Machine: Check hoses for leaks and clean the drum and detergent compartments to prevent mold and odors.
4. Smoke and Carbon Monoxide Detectors: Ensure batteries are working and detectors are functioning properly for safety.
5. Plumbing: Check under sinks, around toilets and near appliances for any leaks that could lead to water damage.
6. Garbage Disposal: Deep clean with baking soda and vinegar then run citrus peels through the disposal to freshen it.
7. Bathtubs and Showers: Replace any cracked or worn caulking to prevent water seepage and mold growth.
8. Attic and Basement: Look for signs of pests like rodents or insects and take preventive measures.
9. Ceiling Fans: Dust and clean ceiling fan blades to improve air circulation and energy efficiency.
10. Refrigerator: Deep clean the inside and discard any expired items. Vacuum or brush the coils behind your refrigerator to improve energy efficiency and prolong the life of the appliance.

By addressing maintenance tasks promptly, homeowners can promote comfort and the longevity of their property while minimizing potential problems.


## FAQ for Homeowner Maintenance Tips


### How often should I follow a home maintenance checklist?


Most homeowners should follow a home maintenance checklist seasonally, with a few tasks checked monthly and others completed annually. Regular maintenance helps catch small issues before they turn into expensive repairs.


A mid-summer review is especially useful because heat, storms and heavy air conditioner use can reveal problems that are easy to miss earlier in the year. Checking gutters, plumbing, weather stripping and cooling systems during summer can help reduce the need for major home maintenance repair later.


### What home maintenance tasks should I prioritize if I have limited time?


If you're short on time, focus on tasks that protect your home from water damage, safety risks and costly equipment failures. Start with gutters, plumbing leaks, HVAC filters, smoke detectors and your home's foundation.


These areas can cause significant damage if overlooked. A clogged gutter, leaking hose or failing air conditioner can quickly lead to repair bills that far exceed the time and cost of regular homeowner maintenance.


### Why is summer a good time for home maintenance repair projects?


Summer typically provides longer daylight hours and better weather conditions, making it easier to inspect and repair both indoor and outdoor areas of your home. Dry conditions can also help reveal issues that may become worse during fall and winter.


This is a good time to address cracked caulking, damaged weather stripping, peeling paint, foundation cracks and sprinkler system problems. Taking care of these repairs now can help prevent moisture intrusion and more expensive damage later in the year.


### How can regular maintenance help me save money as a homeowner?


Regular maintenance can reduce utility costs, extend the life of major systems and help prevent emergency repairs. Small preventive tasks are usually far less expensive than fixing damage after it occurs.


For example, replacing an HVAC filter can improve efficiency, cleaning refrigerator coils can lower energy use and repairing a minor plumbing leak can prevent water damage. A consistent home maintenance routine often protects both your budget and your home's long-term value.


### Does title insurance cover damage caused by poor home maintenance?


No. Title insurance generally does not cover damage caused by deferred maintenance, normal wear and tear or home repair issues that develop after you own the property.


Title insurance is designed to help protect against certain ownership and title-related problems, such as undiscovered liens, recording errors or ownership disputes that existed before you purchased the property. Home maintenance and title insurance work together in different ways to help protect your investment.


### Should I check for permits before starting major home improvement projects?


Yes. Before beginning major renovations, additions, deck construction or other structural projects, it's wise to verify local permit requirements and understand any property restrictions that may apply.


Some projects can be affected by easements, setback requirements or other recorded property rights. Reviewing these details before construction starts can help avoid unexpected delays, disputes or costly changes after work is underway.


### What should I do if a contractor isn't paid for home maintenance or repair work?


Always make sure contractors and subcontractors are paid according to your agreement and keep records of completed payments. Unpaid construction or repair work can sometimes lead to lien claims against a property.


If you're hiring contractors for larger home maintenance repair projects, keep contracts, invoices and lien waivers in your records. Good documentation helps protect homeowners and can make future property transactions smoother.


### How do I protect my home's value beyond regular maintenance?


Protecting your home's value requires both physical upkeep and attention to important ownership records. Maintaining the property, keeping documentation organized and addressing issues promptly all play an important role.


Along with following a home maintenance checklist, homeowners should retain records for repairs, renovations, permits and insurance coverage. Understanding your title insurance policy and maintaining accurate property records can provide another layer of protection for one of your largest investments.
