# Celebrating 30 Years of HGMD: The Gold Standard in Genetic Variant Interpretation

This year marks a major milestone in human genetics: the 30th anniversary of the Human Gene Mutation Database (HGMD). For 3 decades, HGMD has served as a cornerstone resource, cataloging published gene mutations associated with inherited disease and providing researchers and clinicians with a trusted foundation for understanding the genetic basis of human health. What began as an effort to systematically document mutation mechanisms has grown into one of the most comprehensive and widely used repositories of germline disease-causing variation.


Today, HGMD supports a global community of more than 207,000 researchers, clinical laboratories and institutions, playing a critical role in everything from rare disease diagnosis to large-scale genomic research. Its strength lies not only in the breadth of its data, but in its rigorous curation and commitment to scientific accuracy—qualities that have established it as the gold standard in the field.


At this pivotal moment, it’s worth pausing to look back and reflect on how HGMD has evolved alongside the field of genetics itself. From the early days of mutation discovery to the era of next-generation sequencing (NGS) and precision medicine, the database has continuously adapted, expanded and refined its role, helping to shape the way we interpret genetic variation today.


### 30 years of HGMD: A brief history


Over the past 3 decades, the database has evolved alongside the field of genetics itself, adapting to new technologies, expanding scientific knowledge and the growing demands of clinical practice. Its journey reflects not just the progression of a single resource, but the transformation of how we study, interpret and apply genetic variation in human health.


- **1996:** The first public version of HGMD containing approximately 10,000 variants in around 600 genes is made freely available from Cardiff University in April 1996.


- **2000:** As genomic research accelerated in the early 2000s, HGMD evolves rapidly alongside it. By the mid-2000s, the volume of reported mutations surges, with thousands of new entries added each year. This growth transforms HGMD from a research tool into the central, unified repository for germline disease-associated variation. At the same time, its global footprint expands, with hundreds of organizations—spanning clinical diagnostics, public health and commercial genomics—relying on the database to support accurate interpretation and faster patient diagnoses.


- **2006** : HGMD Professional, a stand-alone web application, made available under license from BIOBASE GmbH, is created. HGMD Professional is the subscription-based version, designed specifically for clinical labs, researchers and biotech organizations that need comprehensive, up-to-date information on disease-causing genetic variants. While the public version provides a limited dataset that is 3 years behind, HGMD Professional offers the full, continuously updated collection of curated mutations reported in the scientific literature, along with enhanced annotation and search capabilities.


- **2009** : HGMD contributes to the development of the computational model MutPred at Cardiff University, which predicts changes in protein sequences as a consequence of genetic mutation. First published in 2009, MutPred was independently confirmed to be one of the best performing mutation pathogenicity prediction methods available (1).


- **2015** : In 2015, QIAGEN acquires BIOBASE, and a commercial partnership continues with HGMD data being made available via HGMD Professional (including data download) plus integration into Ingenuity Variant Analysis (IVA, now retired) and QIAGEN Clinical Insight (QCI).


- **2016** : A 2016 survey reveals that HGMD users access the database to save time trawling through literature, to determine if an identified mutation had been previously published, and to quickly find known mutations for a specific gene, solving challenges such as workflow bottlenecks, delayed processing of patient samples and prioritizing correct mutations (1).


- **2017** : Genomics England selects HGMD for its 100,000 Genomes Project (2).


- **2019** : LabCorp, one of the largest clinical laboratory networks in the world, incorporates HGMD within their QCI license in 2019 to improve identification and interpretation of genetic variants within inherited diseases (1).


- **2020** : By the end of 2020, the HGMD contains over 307,000 manually curated mutation reports covering more than 12,000 genes, which have been published in over 3000 peer-reviewed journals. Approximately 30,000 mutation entries are added to the database per annum, and cDNA reference sequences are available for 98% of listed genes.


- **2022** : NHS England and NHS Improvement sign a 2-year licensing agreement with QIAGEN to use HGMD Online Professional across a network of 7 NHS Laboratory Hubs (GLHs) in England (3).


- **2025** :[HGMD Professional is integrated into Franklin by QIAGEN](https://digitalinsights.qiagen.com/franklin-with-hgmd/) , creating one of the most advanced solutions for hereditary disease interpretation. Instead of searching external databases separately, users can access HGMD’s comprehensive, literature-backed mutation data in real time within Franklin. This means known pathogenic variants, supporting evidence and publication links are immediately available alongside other data sources, streamlining analysis, providing unmatched filtering flexibility and reducing the risk of missed or overlooked information.


- **2026** : HGMD Professional surpasses 575,000 expert-curated mutation entries, across 14,517 genes with over 207,000 active users in over 50 countries.


### From database to global gold standard


Over 3 decades, the evolution of HGMD has been by scale, accessibility and integration. Continuous curation, expanding mutation types and the integration of new data sources ensures the database remains aligned with advances in sequencing technologies and precision medicine. Over time, HGMD not only kept pace with the field—it has helped define it, becoming the primary reference for disease-causing genetic variation used by the international biomedical community.


HGMD Professional plays a vital role in accelerating diagnostic workflows across leading research and clinical institutions worldwide. Subscribers include renowned organizations such as Johns Hopkins University, Baylor College of Medicine, Mayo Clinic, MD Anderson Cancer Center, Scripps Research and Children’s Hospital of Philadelphia, all of which rely on HGMD data to support faster, more accurate variant interpretation (1). In the UK, HGMD is widely used across the NHS to aid in diagnosis and the prioritization of candidate genes for disease analysis, with institutions such as Royal Brompton Hospital, Addenbrooke’s Hospital and Great Ormond Street Hospital integrating it into their clinical workflows (1).


Looking ahead, HGMD is poised to become even more essential as genomics enters an increasingly AI-driven era. As automated tools and large-scale data analysis continue to expand, the need for expertly curated, high-confidence reference data will only grow. HGMD’s rigorously validated content positions it as a de facto trusted resource—anchoring interpretation in scientific evidence and helping researchers and clinicians navigate complexity with confidence. Today, HGMD remains a critical foundation for advancing diagnostics, supporting discovery, and ensuring that innovation in genomics is guided by accuracy and trust.
